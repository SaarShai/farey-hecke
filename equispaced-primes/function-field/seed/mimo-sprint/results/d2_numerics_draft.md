## Theorem context (AK Thm 3.4, char p > 0 unconditional)

For K = F_q(T)(ζ_M), G = (F_q[T]/M)*, t = dim_{F_2}(G/G²), and A ∈ G with m(σ_A) = 0:

  LHS_n(A) := π_{1/2,K}(q^n) − Φ(M) · π_{1/2}(q^n; M, A)
            = C(A) · log n + c(A) + o(1)        (n → ∞)

where C(A) = (2^t − 1)/2 if A ∈ G²,  −1/2 otherwise.

Unconditional over function fields by Kaneko–Koyama–Kurokawa.

**Observation (locally derived; the analytic Agent B piece):**
- Average over QR coset (A=1, A=1+T²): C̄_QR = +0.4748 = **exactly Ex 3.6's measured value**.
- Average over non-QR coset: C̄_nonQR = −0.4748 (same magnitude, opposite sign).

This is **not** a deviation from theory. The leading C is identical for both QRs by AK Thm 3.4. The per-class split is a finite-LSQ-window artifact controlled by the order-4 characters χ_1, χ_3 whose L-values are `1/2 ± i(√2−1)/2` (modulus 0.5412). The L-zero phase contributes a (1/n) oscillation whose projection onto the [7, 22] LSQ window gives ~ ±0.03.

Asymptotically (N → ∞), all 4 class slopes converge to ±0.5.

**Honest framing**: "The QR-averaged slope +0.4748 matches Ex 3.6 within numerical accuracy; the within-coset spread is a finite-window LSQ artifact from order-4 character L-zeros, not a deviation from AK Thm 3.4." This is stronger than the cherry-picked A=1 = +0.50449 claim because it explains the structure.

## δ_ff — function-field Rubinstein-Sarnak density

The SESSION.md reports **δ_ff = 1.0000 over n ≤ 22** for both (q=2, M=T²) non-QR vs QR and (q=2, M=T³) A=3 vs A=1. **This requires downgrading.**

Local simulation under the function-field LI null (zeros' phases iid uniform on [0, 2π); conjugate-pair constraint for real quadratic characters), 200,000 trials each:

| Case | E[δ_ff(22) \| null] | stddev | **P(δ_ff(22) = 1 \| null)** | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (q=2, M=T²) | 0.2817 | 0.2669 | **4.15%** | MARGINAL |
| (q=2, M=T³), conjugate-constrained zeros (correct null) | 0.2820 | 0.2673 | **4.14%** | MARGINAL |
| (q=2, M=T³), unconstrained zeros (looser null) | 0.3498 | 0.3300 | **7.96%** | CONSISTENT WITH NULL |

**Verdict**: at N=22 the observed δ_ff = 1.0000 is **marginal** evidence at best. P ≈ 4% under the correct conjugate-constrained null is below the 5% significance threshold but well inside what a 22-point sample can produce by chance. The bimodal null distribution (E ≈ 0.28, stddev ≈ 0.27, large mass at both extremes) means δ_ff at small N is essentially a random-walk-streak statistic, with both δ_ff = 0 and δ_ff = 1 having ~5–20% null probability.

This exactly matches the killed "9×–52× margin" pattern from DPAC PR #3716 — a too-clean number at small sample size that turned out to be a finite-sample artifact.

**Honest paper framing**: "At N = 22 we observe δ_ff = 1 in both tested cases, marginally consistent with the LI null (P ≈ 0.04). A larger N (or independent function-field analog of Rubinstein-Sarnak's 0.9959) would be needed to distinguish a strong-bias signal from null-coincidence."

The asymptotic δ* (long-run fraction) under the null is 1/2 by symmetry — there is no asymptotic bias of one class over another in the unweighted prime count, only finite-degree fluctuations. The SESSION.md framing "δ_ff = 1.0000 (the unconditional analog of R-S's GRH+LI-conditional 0.9959)" therefore **doesn't survive**: R-S's 0.9959 is an asymptotic statement; δ_ff = 1.0000 at n ≤ 22 is finite-sample-streak, not asymptotic.
