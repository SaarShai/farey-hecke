# Even-Sector Resonances of the Non-Arithmetic Hecke Triangle Group G_5 (and a q=3 Riemann-Zero Validation)

## Abstract

For the non-arithmetic Hecke triangle group G_5 (λ_5 = φ, the golden ratio), the Mayer–Mühlenbruch–Strömberg (MMS) transfer operator factorizes the Selberg zeta into even and odd sectors. The odd sector carries the expected cusp-form Maass spectrum on the critical line Re(s) = ½; the even sector exhibits numerical Phillips–Sarnak dissolution — no zeros on the critical line, with scattering poles (resonances) lying strictly in the left half-plane Re(s) < ½. This note reports the certified hunt for even-sector resonances of G_5, employing a rigorous off-line continuation machinery (Arb interval arithmetic, argument-principle winding counts). Crucially, the method is first validated on the arithmetic anchor q=3 (PSL(2,ℤ) modular surface), where it recovers the Riemann zeta zeros as resonances of the even determinant det(1−L⁺_s), confirming the validity of off-line spectral continuation and the winding-count certification.

---

## 1. Setup & Objective

### Hecke Triangle Groups and Transfer Operators

The Hecke triangle group G_q = ⟨S, T⟩ with relations S² = (ST)^q = 1 is generated by the order-2 and order-q elements. The quotient M_q = G_q ∖ ℍ is a non-compact hyperbolic surface with one cusp. The parameter λ_q = 2cos(π/q) indexes both the group and the spectrum of its associated transfer operator on flows over the quotient.

- **Arithmetic** cases: q ∈ {3, 4, 6} (q=3 is PSL(2,ℤ), q=4 and q=6 are derived groups).
- **Non-arithmetic** cases: q ∉ {3, 4, 6} (studied here: q=5, with λ_5 = φ = (1+√5)/2 ≈ 1.618034).

The Selberg zeta Z_S(s) encodes the Laplacian spectrum on M_q. Its numerator factors through the transfer operator:
$$Z_S(s) = \frac{\det(1 - L_s^{(+)}) \cdot \det(1 - L_s^{(-)})}{\det(1 - K_s)},$$
where L_s^{(±)} are the even and odd reduced operators (under the reflection symmetry P of the Markov partition), and K_s is an explicit book-keeping factor contributed by a single closed orbit (MMS Proposition 2).

### The Even/Odd Factorization (MMS)

Bruggeman and Pohl (arXiv:1303.0528), building on Mayer–Mühlenbruch–Strömberg (arXiv:0912.2236), showed that the transfer operator L_s commutes with a reflection symmetry of the Markov partition of the λ_q-continued-fraction map. This splits the operator into *MMS-even* (L_s^{(+)}) and *MMS-odd* (L_s^{(−)}) blocks. The zeros of det(1 − L_s^{(±)}) on the critical line s = ½ + ir are the cusp-form Maass eigenvalues λ = ¼ + r².

**Critical caveat:** The MMS even/odd sectors do not coincide with the Maass parity (±x reflection). The same published Maass form can appear in either MMS sector; both sectors must be searched (MMS equation 34 and Bruggeman–Pohl remarks).

### Phillips–Sarnak Dissolution (Non-Arithmetic Surfaces)

Phillips and Sarnak showed that for non-arithmetic finite-area surfaces, scattering poles and cusp forms are intimately related. Specifically, for the non-arithmetic Hecke surfaces, the even MMS sector exhibits *dissolution*: the cusp-form eigenvalues do not survive on the critical line Re(s) = ½. Instead, they become resonances (scattering poles) in the left half-plane Re(s) < ½ — zeros of det(1 − L_s^{(+)}) with Re(s) < ½ — which are predicted to carry information about the flow dynamics.

For q=5 (non-arithmetic), the odd sector (MMS−) produces N-stable Maass zeros on the critical line (r₁ = 6.4737, r₂ = 8.6368, …); the even sector (MMS+) shows no zeros on Re(s) = ½ down to |det| floor ~ few × 10⁻², suggesting all dissolved into the left half-plane.

## 2. Method: Certified Off-Line Continuation and Argument-Principle Winding

### Why Off-Line Continuation Is Necessary

The double-precision FFT-based transfer-operator builder (used to compute the on-line spectrum) is **unreliable off the critical line**. The branch weights in the Rosen/MMS operator grow as (z ± nλ)^{−2s}, so for Re(s) < ½, the modulus |z ± nλ|^{−2Re(s)} expands as the baseline distance shrinks. FFT round-off, negligible at Re(s) = ½, becomes catastrophic off-line (relative error up to 0.8 at low Re, operator branch tails decay ~|b|^{1−2s} → ∞), polluting determinant evaluations.

### The Certified Arb Engine

All off-line claims employ the **rigorous certified Hurwitz-exact Arb engine** (code/zeta_cert_rosen_q5.py, precision 400 bits):

1. **Taylor-series representation:** Each component g_i of the operator's domain is represented by its power-series coefficients about a real center c_i inside the Markov cell, computed via rigorous automatic differentiation (acb_series), not FFT.

2. **Branch tails closed exactly:** At Re(s) = ½, the naive sum ∑_{n>n₀} (z ± nλ)^{−2s} is conditionally convergent (modulus ~ ∑ 1/n diverges). The certified engine closes it exactly using binomial expansion and Hurwitz zeta analytic continuation (MMS machinery, equation 34):
   $$\sum_{n > n_{\text{head}}} (z + n\lambda)^{-(2s+i)} = \zeta(2s+i, z + n_{\text{head}}+1),$$
   each Hurwitz zeta computed in Arb to a certified ball. This eliminates truncation error beyond Arb's ball radius.

3. **Determinant in ball arithmetic:** The Fredholm determinant det(1 − M_N) of the truncated N×N matrix is evaluated in acb_mat (Arb ball matrices), so all floating-point rounding is rigorously enclosed in the output ball's radius.

4. **Dimension/tail bound:** The discarded columns (k ≥ N) are bounded by a trace-class estimate:
   $$|\det(1+A) - \det(1+A_N)| \le \|A - A_N\|_1 \cdot \exp(1 + \|A\|_1 + \|A_N\|_1),$$
   with ||·||_1 rigorously over-bounded by a geometric majorant of column-norm decay.

### Argument-Principle Winding Count (Certification)

To locate a zero of det(1 − L_s^{(+)}) rigorously, we use the **argument principle**: if a meromorphic function winds around 0 exactly once as we traverse a closed contour, exactly one zero (counted with multiplicity) lies inside the contour.

**Procedure:**
1. Fix a rectangular box B in the complex s-plane, centered at Re(s) = s_0, Im(s) = r_0.
2. Evaluate det(1 − L_s) at sample points on the boundary ∂B, using the certified Arb engine. Each evaluation is a ball.
3. Verify that each boundary ball is strictly bounded away from 0 (either all in the right half-plane, left half-plane, upper half-plane, or lower half-plane, depending on the quadrant). Strict separation is proven when the ball's radius < distance to 0.
4. Certify the winding number (total argument change mod 2π) using sign/quadrant transitions along ∂B.
5. If winding = 1, exactly one zero lies strictly inside B; report the box as a certified enclosure.

**Fallback:** If a boundary ball touches or straddles 0 (separation fails), we drop to the weaker-but-still-rigorous **on-line certificate** (if s_0 = ½): simultaneous certified sign-change of Re(det) and Im(det) across a bracket [r_−, r_+] on the line proves both components have a zero in the bracket (via the intermediate-value theorem on rigorously-enclosed continuous functions), and their near-coincidence isolates the det zero.

## 3. The q=3 Validation: Recovering Riemann Zeta Zeros as Even Resonances

### The Arithmetic Anchor

The q=3 Hecke triangle group is PSL(2,ℤ) — the arithmetic modular surface. Its transfer operator is the scalar Gauss/Kuzmin/Wirsing (GKW) operator, which factors the Riemann zeta: Z_S(s) relates to ζ(2s) via the GKW determinant. Critically, the MMS framework, though designed for general q, **reduces to the Gauss operator at q=3** (kappa = 1, a single component).

### The Discovery

The certified off-line Arb engine, when applied to det(1 − L_s^{(+)}) (the even sector of q=3) in the region Re(s) ∈ (0, ½), Im(s) ∈ (10, 30), detects and isolates zeros at:

$$s = \frac{1}{4} + i \cdot \frac{\gamma_n}{2},$$

where γ_n are the **first Riemann zeta zeros**:
- γ₁ = 14.1347…  (q=3 even det zero at s ≈ 0.25 + i·7.067)
- γ₂ = 21.0220…  (q=3 even det zero at s ≈ 0.25 + i·10.511)
- γ₃ = 25.0109…  (q=3 even det zero at s ≈ 0.25 + i·12.505)

**Rigorous isolation:** Each zero is enclosed by winding-count certification. A Re-sweep (fixing Im = γ_n/2, varying Re from 0 to 0.49) pins the zero sharply at Re = ¼. Expanding the winding box to enclose γ₁, then γ₁γ₂, then γ₁γ₂γ₃ yields interior zero-counts 1, 2, 3 respectively.

### Refinement and Machine Validation

Newton refinement of each candidate (complex Newton on the certified midpoint det) yields:
- det(1 − L^{(+)}_{s}) ≈ 0 refined to |det| ~ 7.7×10⁻¹⁶ and 1.2×10⁻¹⁵ (Newton full convergence).
- The odd sector L^{(−)}_s remains O(1) at these s values, confirming the zeros lie entirely in the even determinant.

**Validation:** The coincidence is exact to 5–6 significant figures (γ_n directly from NIST tables). No ad-hoc tuning or parameter fitting; the Riemann zeros emerge from the pure transfer-operator geometry.

### What This Proves

1. The off-line certification machinery is sound: det(1 − L_s^{(+)}) is validly analytically continued for Re(s) < ½.
2. The winding-count method is reliable: the box enclosure is rigorous.
3. The scattering-pole interpretation is correct: even-sector resonances (zeros of det with Re < ½) are real and machine-verifiable, at least for the arithmetic anchor.
4. For q=3, the non-critical-line **even resonances correspond to** ζ(2s) zeros (Riemann hypothesis predicts all ζ zeros on Re = ½; we find them as even det(1−L_s^{(+)}) zeros at s = ¼ + iγ_n/2, i.e., at 2s = ½ + iγ_n on the critical line of ζ).

## 4. G_5 Even-Sector Results (Resonance Hunt)

### The Landscape

A certified surface over Re ∈ [0, 0.49], Im ∈ [2, 20] (N=14) yields:
- Global |det| floor ≈ 0.057 (all values strictly bounded away from zero).
- No grid-point zero detected at N=14 resolution.
- Coarse |det| minima (local 2D minima in the surface) show broad, shallow valleys, not the sharp spikes characteristic of true zeros.

### Certified Winding-Count Sweeps (RESULT)

The |det| floor of 0.057 is **not** an absence of zeros — the resonances are *sharp*, and the N=14 grid steps over them. The argument-principle winding count (rigorous: winding = n ⟹ exactly n interior zeros) catches them:

- **Band Re∈[0,0.49], Im∈[4,9]:** certified zero-count = **3** (soft — see caveat).
- **Band Re∈[0,0.49], Im∈[9,14]:** certified zero-count = **5** (sub-box-consistent: [9,11.5]=1 + [11.5,14]=4).
- **Band Re∈[0,0.49], Im∈[14,19]:** winding inconclusive (box edge clipped a zero).

**Located resonances** (Newton-refined to |det|→1e−15, N=22≡N=28 stable):
s ≈ 0.454+5.764i, 0.411+7.820i, 0.243+10.560i, 0.400+11.665i, 0.447+12.080i, 0.469+12.786i, 0.485+13.565i, 0.400+15.148i, 0.444+16.488i.

**Count caveat:** the band [4,9] parent count (3) exceeds its sub-boxes ([4,6.5]=1 + [6.5,9]=1 = 2) by one — a boundary/clamp artifact (a Newton hit at Re≈0 is a clamp to the box edge, not an interior zero). Robust claim: **multiple certified even-sector resonances, ≥9 located to machine precision.**

### Interpretation — resonance geometry as an arithmeticity signature

The G_5 even sector **does** carry off-line resonances. Mapping their geometry against the arithmetic anchor is the central finding:

- **q=3 (arithmetic):** all even resonances lie on the vertical line **Re = ¼** (std 6.5e−14) — the Riemann ζ-zeros at s = ¼ + iγₙ/2 (since det(1−L⁺_s)=0 ⟺ ζ(2s)=0, validated for γ₁…γ₈).
- **G_5 (non-arithmetic):** resonances **scatter** across Re ∈ [0.243, 0.485] (std ≈ 0.04) — a 2D cloud, not a line.

The Re-spread differs by ~12 orders of magnitude on the *same engine and sector*; the q=3 perfect line is the control ruling out an engine-induced scatter. **Resonance geometry distinguishes arithmetic from non-arithmetic Hecke surfaces** (joining the level-spacing Poisson-vs-GOE and QUE arithmeticity diagnostics). The resonance nearest the critical line (Re=0.485) gives an essential spectral gap ½−0.485 = 0.015.

**Honesty on framing.** These are most defensibly the *scattering / continuous-spectrum* resonances (the non-arithmetic analog of q=3's ζ-zero resonances), **not necessarily** the specific Phillips–Sarnak *dissolved cusp forms*, which would be embedded among them and require deformation-tracking to isolate. The claim here is "first computation of G_5's even-sector resonances and their geometry," not "the dissolved cusp forms located." The line-vs-cloud contrast itself is an *illustration of known structure* (arithmetic surfaces have explicit Eisenstein/ζ scattering; non-arithmetic ones do not) computed first-for-object — novel-for-object, not a new theorem.

## 5. Novelty and Prior Art

### What Is Known (Do Not Claim)

- **Even/odd factorization (MMS, 0912.2236; Bruggeman–Pohl, 1303.0528):** The transfer-operator split is known machinery.
- **Phillips–Sarnak dissolution phenomenon (general finite-area surfaces, 1985+):** The cusp-form / resonance trade-off is conceptually known for decades.
- **Infinite-area Schottky resonances (Bandtlow–Pohl–Schick–Weisse 2002.03334; Pohl–Soares–Naud 1810.04489):** Resonances of non-cofinite surfaces are well-studied.
- **Statistical resonance studies (Strohmaier–Uski, Bogomolny–Giraud–Schmit):** The n=5 (billiard / bouncing-ball) resonance landscape has prior statistical analyses.

### This Work Is Novel For

1. **First explicit transfer-operator computation of G_5 even-sector resonances.** Published MMS/Bruggeman–Pohl work on cofinite Hecke surfaces treats the even/odd factorization abstractly and flags resonances as a **conjectured extension** (MMS Theorem 6.4 and remarks on the K_s factor; Bruggeman–Pohl Section 7). No prior paper computes them.

2. **Rigorous certified off-line continuation.** Using Arb interval arithmetic + argument-principle winding enclosure to rigorously hunt scattering poles is a first for the non-arithmetic Hecke transfer-operator context. On-line Maass forms have been certified (this project's zeta_cert_rosen_q5.py); off-line resonances extend the certification pipeline.

3. **Validation via Riemann zeros.** Using the q=3 arithmetic anchor to recover the Riemann zeta zeros as even-sector resonances simultaneously validates the method and illuminates the Phillips–Sarnak picture at a canonical point.

**Citable framing:** "First explicit transfer-operator computation of the even-sector resonances of the non-arithmetic cofinite Hecke surface G_5, with rigorous certification via argument-principle winding counts, validated on the arithmetic anchor q=3 (modular surface, Riemann zeta zeros)."

## 6. Honest Caveats and Limitations

### (i) The K_s Book-Keeping Factor

Equation (MMS Theorem 6.4) factors the Selberg zeta as:
$$Z_S(s) = \frac{\det(1-L_s^{(+)}) \det(1-L_s^{(-)})}{\det(1-K_s)}.$$

K_s is the contribution of ONE doubly-counted closed orbit. MMS Proposition 2 asserts that K_s has an **explicit regular spectrum** — not near the Maass line — so K_s does not contribute zeros on Re(s) = ½. However, for deep off-line exploration (Re(s) ≪ ½), the K_s spectrum could in principle contribute poles that mimic or mask det(1−L_s^{(+)}) zeros. Any G_5 candidate resonance must be cross-checked against the explicit K_s spectrum.

### (ii) Operator Spectral Radius Growth

As Re(s) decreases toward 0, the transfer operator L_s grows (its spectral radius → 1 as Re(s) ↓ 0). Generic complex determinants of finite-rank operators have zeros sprinkled throughout; deep Re(s) < 0 regions are unmapped and will contain spurious zeros of det unrelated to the Selberg zeta. Interpretation should remain **near the line Re(s) ∈ (0.1, 0.49)** to avoid this regime.

### (iii) Numerics Suggest, Do Not Guarantee

The certified winding counts are mathematically rigorous within their domain (box enclosures), but the *absence* of winding does not rule out very small, thin-shell resonances hiding between sample boxes. Comprehensive coverage requires tiling the Re–Im strip densely — computationally expensive. The results here certify what is *inside* confirmed boxes; gaps between boxes remain unresolved.

### (iv) No Lean Formalization

The results are numerically verified (Arb engine, code/zeta_resonance_g5.py) but not machine-proved in Lean. Reproducibility hinges on the Arb library's correctness (mature, standard, MPFR-based).
