**Preceding context (15+ lines before):**

To correct for this overcounting we introduce another transfer operator, $\mathcal{K}_s$, whose Fredholm

$$Z_S(s)=\frac{\text{det}\,(1-\mathcal{L}_s)}{\text{det}\,(1-\mathcal{K}_s)}=\frac{\text{det}\,[(1-\mathcal{L}_{s,+})(1-\mathcal{L}_{s,-})]}{\text{det}\,(1-\mathcal{K}_s)},$$

This is the key factorization showing the determinant splits as det[(1-L_{s,+})(1-L_{s,-})].

ITEM 2c: PARITY/REFLECTION OPERATOR P AND ± SECTORS
================================================================================

**Definition of P (lines 1528–1531):**

\begin{equation*}
\label{P}
 (P\underline{f})_i(z):= f_{-i}(-z) \quad\text{for}\quad \underline{f}= (f_i)_{i\in A_{\kappa_q}}.
\end{equation*}

**Property:** $P^2 = \text{id}_B$, i.e., P is an involution.

**Commutativity with transfer operator (Lemma, lines 1535–1552):**

The operators $P:B\to B$ and $\mathcal{L}_s:B\to B$ commute for all $s\in \mathbb{C}\setminus \{s_1,s_2,\ldots\}$.

**Eigenspace decomposition and ± sector definition (lines 1554–1556):**

The previous lemma allows us to restrict the operator $\mathcal{L}_s$ to the eigenspaces of the operator $P$. Since this is a linear
 involution it can only have eigenvalues $\pm 1$. Denote the corresponding eigenspaces by $B_\pm$. Then
$\underline{f}=(f_i)_{i\in _{A\kappa_q}}\in B_{\pm}$ if and only if $f_{-i}(-z)=\pm \,f_i(z)$ for $ i \in A_{\kappa_q}$.

**Reduced operators on ± sectors (lines 1554–1560):**

Let $B_{\kappa_q}$ denote the Banach space $B_{\kappa_q}=\oplus_{1\leq i\leq\kappa_q} B(D_i)$ with
the discs $D_i$ as defined earlier in Lemma~\ref{E1.5}. Then the transfer operator $\mathcal{L}_s$
restricted to the spaces $B_\pm $ induces operators
$\mathcal{L}_{s,\pm} $ on the Banach space $B_{\kappa_q}$.

SUMMARY OF STRUCTURE
================================================================================

The paper establishes (Theorem at lines 1971–1977):

Main Theorem:
$$Z_S(s) = \frac{\text{det}(1-\mathcal{L}_s)}{\text{det}(1-\mathcal{K}_s)} = \frac{\det[(1-\mathcal{L}_{s,+})(1-\mathcal{L}_{s,-})]}{\text{det}(1-\mathcal{K}_s)}$$

Key structural insights:
1. The parity operator P commutes with L_s (Lemma line 1535–1552).
2. This commutativity allows spectral block-decomposition into + (even) and − (odd) sectors.
3. The factorization det(1−L_+)det(1−L_−) follows from simultaneous diagonalizability.
4. The operator L_s is written as a 2κ_q × 2κ_q matrix (Theorem E6, lines 1476–1505).
5. The reduced operators L_{s,±} act on B_κ_q (dimension κ_q) and satisfy explicit finite-rank recursion formulas (lines 1562–1585).
