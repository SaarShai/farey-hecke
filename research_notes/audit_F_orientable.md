## VERDICT: **SOFT (leaning) — confidence 55%.** The single best candidate seen so far,
but with one genuine TIGHT red flag that caps confidence below "clearly soft."

The record (cyclic binary OS(8) = 92) was set by a **heuristic backtracking
extension search, NOT exhaustive and NOT proven optimal** (S1 = SOFT). The exact
combinatorial object is **tiny and SAT/ILP-tractable** (de Bruijn graph B₂(7) has
only 128 vertices / 256 edges), and **no SAT/ILP/exhaustive attack on the small-order
cyclic record has ever been run** (S4 = SOFT — re-confirmed). It is unowned, unbeaten,
and O(period·n)-verifiable (S5 = SOFT). The drag is **S2/S3**: the gap is only 92→96=**4**,
the bound is the *sharp* Dai bound (not trivial), and the structurally-analogous order n=7
is **proven-exhaustively maximal at 36, four below its Dai bound of 40** — i.e. the Dai bound
is loose by exactly 4 right where the truth is known, so the true OS(8) max may well be
**92 or 93**, leaving room for at most +1 or +2. That is acute-set-adjacent. It is NOT the
acute-set trap (record there was an exact product-construction value; here it is an admitted
heuristic), but the *upside is small*.

**One-line decision:** worth the ONE cheap probe below before any fleet; if the probe
yields an exhaustive/SAT settlement at n=8 (proves 92 optimal OR finds 93–95), that is a
genuine new fact regardless of which way it lands. Fleet only conditional on that probe.

## The object (precise, to avoid the conflation trap)
A **(cyclic) orientable sequence of order n**, OS(n): a *periodic* binary sequence in which
every length-n window (n-tuple) occurs **at most once per period in EITHER reading
direction**. M_n = max period. We audit the **binary (k=2), cyclic** case at n=8 (and 9,10).

Two adjacent objects exist and MUST NOT be conflated (I caught a web summary doing so):
- **Acyclic orientable sequences (AOS)** — different, longer: AOS(8)=108 [debruijnsequence.org].
  NOT our target.
- **k-ary (k≥3) and "negative" orientable sequences** — different objects, separate tables.
  The 2025 Mitchell–Wild "93/372/1390/7763" records are **ternary k=3 / k≥3**, NOT binary.
  (I nearly mis-attributed "93" as a beaten binary OS(8); it is OS₃(5). See S5.)

the binary content of arXiv:2401.14341, Gabrić–Sawada), **Table 1**, extracted verbatim:

| n  | Rₙ (recursive constr.) | Lₙ | **L\*ₙ (longest known)** | Uₙ (Dai upper bound) | status |
|----|----|----|----|----|----|
| 5  | –  | 0  | **6**   | 6   | record MEETS bound → **proven max = 6**  |
| 6  | –  | 6  | **16**  | 17  | proven max = 16 (gap 1)                  |
| 7  | –  | 14 | **36**  | 40  | **proven max = 36** (exhaustive) → bound loose by **4** |
| 8  | 80 | 48 | **92**  | 96  | **longest-known, NOT proven** (gap 4)    |
| 9  | 161| 126| **174** | 206 | longest-known (gap 32)                    |
| 10 | 322| 300| **416** | 443 | longest-known (gap 27)                    |

Cross-check: the abstract states "Maximal length orientable sequences are known only for
n ≤ 7" (KNOWN, quoted). n=6,7 maxima "were shown to be optimally long by exhaustive search"
(KNOWN — Mitchell talk + Gabrić; the n=5 record meets the bound exactly).

## S1 — weak record method?  **SOFT (high confidence).**
**HOW 92 was actually found — quoted verbatim** from Gabrić §4 "Extending orientable sequences"
(arXiv:1911.11704, extracted):

> "The values from the column labeled L\*ₙ … were found by extending an OS(n) of length Lₙ
> constructed in the previous section. … 1. For each index i, apply a **standard backtracking
> search** to see whether … can be extended to a longer OS(n). We followed several
> **heuristics** … This approach was **fairly successful for even n**, but found shorter
> extensions for n odd. … 2. … starting from the sequences generated by recursion, we again
> apply the **exhaustive search to find minor extensions (the depth of recursion is
> significantly reduced)**."

So 92 = (recursive Mitchell–Wild base sequence, Lₙ=48, Rₙ=80) **+ greedy/heuristic
backtracking extension with bounded depth**. This is explicitly **NOT a global exhaustive
search**, **NOT a proven optimum**, **NOT SAT/ILP**. It is precisely a "weak / non-exhaustive
method with obvious slack" — the strongest softness signal in the rubric. The authors even
flag the heuristic worked *better for even n* (8 is even) but is depth-limited.
**S1 verdict: SOFT.**

## S2 — wide gap?  **TIGHT-leaning (the main drag).**
Gap 92 → **96 is only 4**, and 96 is the **sharp Dai bound**, NOT the trivial one. KNOWN:
- Trivial bound 2^(n-1) − 2^⌊(n-1)/2⌋ = 128 − 8 = **120** at n=8.
- Dai–Martin–Robshaw–Wild (1993) sharpened it to a 4-case formula giving **U₈ = 96** (verified;
  the formula and Table 1 extracted). 96 < 120, so the binding bound is already tightened.
- **The 2025 Mitchell–Wild "improved upper bounds" do NOT help here**: their Theorem 2.22 gives
  k=2, n=8 = **105** (their Table 1, k=2 column, extracted) — *weaker* (higher) than Dai's 96.
  Their improvement is for the **general-alphabet** machinery; for binary the Dai bound 96 remains
  the best known. (So "best known upper bound for binary OS(8) = 96", current.)

Adversarial read: at n=7 the **exhaustively-proven max is 36, while the Dai bound is 40** — the
Dai bound is **loose by exactly 4** right where we know the answer. If OS(8) behaves the same,
the true M₈ is plausibly **92 or 93**, and the record may already be optimal or off by 1. The
real headroom is small (+1 to +4, most likely +0/+1). **S2 verdict: TIGHT-leaning.** This is
the one criterion that keeps the overall verdict from being "clearly SOFT."

## S3 — TIGHT red flags?  **MIXED → net mild-soft.**
- Record ≠ clean closed-form / product / construction value? **GOOD (soft):** 92 is a
  *heuristic search output*, not a formula value. (Contrast acute sets, where 33 was an exact
  product-construction value — that trap does NOT apply here.)
- Nearby sizes proven optimal? **RED FLAG (tight):** n=5,6,7 are **proven maximal** (6,16,36) by
  exhaustive search. Proven-optimal immediate neighbours is a classic tightness signal and the
  reason S2 is loose-by-only-4.
- Multiply-stability-certified / on a cultivated record table? **PARTIAL:** it IS on a maintained
  specialist table (debruijnsequence.org, Sawada's group) — mild tightness — but that table is
  explicitly **"longest known," not optimal**, and is refreshed by *their own heuristic*, not an
  independent exhaustive/SAT certification. No "multiply-certified-stable across methods/years"
  story like the acute set's 8-year product-construction lock.
**S3 verdict: net mild-soft** — no closed-form lock and the value is a search output, but
proven-optimal neighbours are a real caution.

## S4 — neglected?  **SOFT (re-confirmed).**
- No SAT / ILP / constraint-solver attack on the small-order **cyclic** record exists in the
  literature (searched; none found). The only "exhaustive" work is (a) the n≤7 maxima (1990s/
  small) and (b) Gabrić's *bounded-depth* extension exhaustive step — neither is a full
  exact attack on n=8.
- No AlphaEvolve / FunSearch / CPro1 / OpenEvolve / RGCS attempt found on OS(n).
- Activity that DOES exist is **construction-theoretic** (asymptotic-optimality:
  Gabrić–Sawada 2024, Mitchell–Wild 2025 arXiv:2507.02526, arXiv:2603.18646; non-binary
  arXiv:2407.14866; negative-OS bounds arXiv:2602.04433) — all aimed at **large-n asymptotics
  or new alphabets**, none at **exactly settling small binary n=8/9/10**. The small-order exact
  question is genuinely a side-eddy. **S4 verdict: SOFT.**

## S5 — still a NEW fact if beaten?  **SOFT (verified current).**
- **Unbeaten NOW:** binary cyclic OS(8) = **92** is current. The most recent paper that could
  have beaten it — Mitchell–Wild, *New orientable sequences*, arXiv:2507.02526v1 (3 Jul 2025) —
  **does NOT improve the binary record**; its new records ("93,372,1390,7763 … come from this
  paper") are **k=3/k≥3 (ternary+)**, and for binary it explicitly defers to Gabrić–Sawada.
  (Quoted: "The OS₃(5), OS₄(5), OS₅(5) and OS₇(5), … come from this paper.") So the "93" is NOT
  binary OS(8). **Binary OS(8)=92 stands.**
- **Unowned:** no exact-value / optimality result claimed by anyone for n=8.
- **Exactly verifiable:** a candidate of period m is checked in O(m·n) by sliding all length-8
  windows and confirming no window or its reverse repeats — trivial to certify.
- **A new fact would be any of:** (i) a new **longest** cyclic binary OS(8) ≥ 93 (≤96);
  (ii) **settling** M₈ exactly (prove 92 is maximal, or prove a specific 93/94/95/96 max);
  (iii) likewise improving/settling **n=9 (174→…→206)** or **n=10 (416→…→443)** — these have
  **much wider gaps** (32 and 27) and so larger upside, though larger search.
  Each is modest but genuine and FunSearch/AlphaEvolve-shaped.
**S5 verdict: SOFT.**

## THE SINGLE CHEAPEST PROBE (< ~1 hr) — do this BEFORE any fleet
**Run an exact solver on cyclic binary OS(8) and see what it returns.** This is uniquely cheap
here because the object is *small*: orientable sequences of order 8 ↔ closed walks in the
de Bruijn graph **B₂(7)** with **128 vertices and 256 edges**, under the "use a window XOR its
reverse, at most once" constraint. That is squarely **SAT/ILP/CP-tractable** (cf. the n≤7 maxima
were settled by exhaustive search decades ago on weaker hardware).

Concretely (one of):
- **(a) SAT/ILP:** variables = which of the 120 reverse-pair windows (256 windows − 16
  self-palindromic, paired) are used + Eulerian/closed-walk consistency on B₂(7); maximize
  period m. Push m from 92 upward (m=93,94,95,96) and ask the solver: SAT (→ **new record**) or
  UNSAT up to 96 (→ **92 proven maximal = new fact, settles n=8**). A 30-60 min CP-SAT/Gurobi run.
- **(b) Cheaper falsification first (~15 min):** reproduce the 92 record *from scratch* with a
  basic greedy + backtracking searcher (no recursion seed). **If a plain searcher reaches 92
  quickly → slack is real, the record is weak → proceed to (a)/fleet.** **If it cannot even
  reach ~88–92 → the record is "strong"/hard-to-touch by naive means → downgrade to UNCERTAIN/
  TIGHT and do NOT fleet** (the value would then be effectively defended by search difficulty).

**Interpretation gate (decide fleet here):**
- (a) returns a sequence of period ≥93  → **SOFT confirmed, new record, fleet justified.**
- (a) returns UNSAT through 96          → **92 is the true maximum** — a clean new fact (settles
  n=8); no *record* fleet, but the settlement itself is the deliverable. STOP, write it up.
- (a) times out but (b) easily hits 92  → **SOFT, fleet the SAT/CP attack** (n=8 first, then the
  wider-gap n=9 with gap 32).
- (b) cannot reach 92                   → **record is strong; reclassify UNCERTAIN→TIGHT, no fleet.**

Either SAT-side outcome at n=8 produces a genuine new fact, so the probe is *positive-EV
regardless of direction* — the cleanest property a softness target can have.

## Caveats / honesty ledger
- "92" and "96" are KNOWN (Gabrić Table 1, extracted verbatim). The goal's "92 vs 96" framing is
  **correct for binary cyclic OS(8)** (an earlier confusion: the *trivial* bound is 120 and the
  2025 general-alphabet bound is 105 — neither is the binding one; Dai's 96 is).
- The biggest near-miss in this audit: the 2025 Mitchell–Wild "93" is **ternary, not binary** —
  the binary record is NOT beaten. Verified by extracting their Tables 1/6/7 (all start at k=3).
- n=9,10 records (174, 416) are similarly heuristic and have **wider gaps** (to 206, 443) → more
  upside but bigger search; if n=8 SAT is easy, n=9 is the natural next fleet target.
- This is a *cyclic* OS audit; AOS(8)=108 and negative-OS are different objects — kept separate.
