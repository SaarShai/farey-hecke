## Attacks that landed — where the first referee and I differ

**A. Attribution. The d=2 counting theorem is Selberg's, not a Kelmer replay.** Kelmer §4 opening: *"generalizing the results of [Sel90] on the distribution of poles of Eisenstein series to hyperbolic manifolds of higher dimensions. Our proof is very similar to Selberg's original proof… The only new difficulty is coming from the fact that in higher dimensions these Dirichlet series have a faster growth rate on the critical line."* Remark 0.2: *"For hyperbolic surfaces this result is due to Selberg [Sel90]."* At d=2 there is no new difficulty and no generalization. `[Sel90]` = Selberg, *Remarks on the distribution of poles of Eisenstein series*, Piatetski-Shapiro Festschrift, Israel Math. Conf. Proc. 3 (1990). Neither the candidate nor the first referee reports either sentence; the first referee names Sel90 only to say it was unavailable, and treats Kelmer as the primary. Before any paper-level use the note must cite Selberg 1990 as the source of the d=2 statement and state plainly that it was not read. Favourable side note: Kelmer says Selberg's setting uses Dirichlet series **with positive coefficients**; for κ=1 the Hejhal (7.5) coefficients are positive integers, so the Hecke case sits inside Selberg's original hypothesis class rather than at its edge.

**B. Prior art inside the candidate's own PDF was not reported.** Hejhal printed pp. 577–579, in the same 33-page scan both the candidate and the first referee inspected: **THEOREM 7.11** — *"Given t₀ ∈ ℝ and 0<δ<1. The rectangle [½, ½+δ] × [t₀−δ, t₀+δ] must contain ZEROS of φ_N(s) whenever N is sufficiently large. It is understood here that N < ∞."* — and **COROLLARY 7.12** — *"The set … contains poles of φ_N(s) whenever N is sufficiently large. Proof. Trivial."* with *"This result corresponds to Selberg[2, page 91 line 5]."* Both documents cite only pp. 568–569 and 574. This is a partial antecedent, weaker in q-range (large N) and stronger in localization (any prescribed height, arbitrarily close to the line). It does not damage the LAW; it does mean the source sweep was incomplete on the decisive novelty question.

**C. A second printed-constant error in Kelmer, missed by the first referee.** The first referee found that Kelmer's post-(4.22) `A_Γ = 2(C_Γ + Σ(σ_j−α)) + B_Γ` is wrong. I confirm independently: expanding `F(T)=aT²logT+BT²+DT+O(log T)` gives `F(T+1)−F(T) = 2aT log T + (a+2B)T + O(log T)`, so `A_Γ = a + 2B_Γ` and the `D` term (which carries `C_Γ` and the pole sum) cancels exactly. But there is a **second** error the first referee did not catch. Kelmer's (4.18), printed p. 18, evaluates
`(νκ/π)∫₀^T (T−t) log|Γ(½+it)/Γ(it)| dt = (νκ/2π)∫₀^T (T−t) log| t tanh(πt) / π | dt`
— the `/π` inside the logarithm is spurious, since `|Γ(½+it)/Γ(it)|² = t tanh(πt)` exactly. It propagates the term `+νκ log(π)/(4π)` into his `B_Γ`. Check at d=2, κ=1, `a_Γ=1/√π`: true `B = (−2log π − 3)/(8π) = −0.2104609172`, matching the numerically evaluated integral to 8+ figures at T = 200/1000/5000; Kelmer's printed `B_Γ = (−4log π − 1)/(8π) = −0.2219781556`. With the true B, `A = a + 2B = −(1+log π)/(2π) = −0.34134436293`, which equals the Riemann–von Mangoldt value for q=3 to 12 digits. With Kelmer's printed B, `A = −0.364378840`, which is wrong. His `C_Γ = −νκ/16` is correct (`∫₀^∞ log tanh x dx = −π²/8`, verified). **Consequence for the LAW: none.** The candidate consumes only the `T log T` coefficient and the bare existence of `A_q`, and explicitly declines the printed `A_Γ` formula and the `B_q, C_q` values. Its conservatism is vindicated by this finding. Anyone who later reaches for `B_q` or `A_q` from Kelmer will be wrong.
