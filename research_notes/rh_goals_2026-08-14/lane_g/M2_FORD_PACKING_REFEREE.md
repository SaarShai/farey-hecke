## Exact theorem and hypotheses

Let \(\Gamma\leq\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)\) be discrete and
non-elementary.  Normalize the selected cusp to

\[
 S:z\mapsto z+1,\qquad
 \Gamma_\infty=\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma(\infty)=\langle S\rangle,
 \qquad H_\infty=\{z:\operatorname{Im}z>1\}.
\]

The authoritative theorem is Caroline Series, *A Crash Course on Kleinian
Groups*, Theorem 2.21 and Lemma 2.22 (the latter is Shimizu's lemma),
Rendiconti dell'Istituto di Matematica dell'Universita di Trieste 37 (2005),
published PDF p. 14:

<https://rendiconti.dmi.units.it/volumi/37/01.pdf>

An ICTP copy is <https://indico.ictp.it/event/a04195/session/7/contribution/5/material/0/0.pdf>.
Series defines precise invariance by
\(g(H_s)=H_s\) for \(g\in P\) and \(g(H_s)\cap H_s=\varnothing\) for
\(g\notin P\).  Theorem 2.21 says that the **interior** of the height-one
horoball is precisely invariant under the parabolic subgroup fixing \(\infty\).
Lemma 2.22 says that if
\(A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in G\) and
\(B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in G\), then
\(|c|\geq1\) for non-elementary discrete \(G\).  Its proof uses Jorgensen's
inequality and \(\operatorname{tr}[A,B]=2+c^2\) (Series PDF text around
lines 431–483 in the web extraction).  No membership in
\(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)\) is used; a subgroup of \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)\)
is a Kleinian subgroup, so the theorem restricts to the invariant copy of
\(\mathbb H^2\).

For the Hecke family, Pohl, *Symbolic dynamics, automorphic functions, and
Selberg zeta functions with unitary representations*, arXiv:1503.00525v3,
§2.2, PDF pp. 5–6, gives the family source:

<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.00525>

It defines \(\Gamma_\lambda=\langle z\mapsto-1/z,z\mapsto z+\lambda\rangle\),
states that it is Fuchsian (discrete) iff
\(\lambda\geq2\) or \(\lambda=2\cos(\pi/q)\), \(q\geq3\), and gives the
fundamental domain.  For finite \(q\), \(\Gamma_\lambda\backslash\mathbb H\)
has one cusp at \(\infty\); \(\lambda=2\) is the theta group and has two
cusps.  Every cusp stabilizer is generated by one parabolic, with
\(P_\infty=T_\lambda=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\lambda\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
The cofinite triangle-orbifold/lattice statement (or equivalently the
\((2,q,\infty)\) presentation) supplies non-elementarity; this is an
inference, not an extra arithmetic assumption.  Moller--Pohl,
arXiv:1103.5235, §2.1, records
\(\langle T,S\mid S^2=1=(TS)^q\rangle\) and the same matrix generators:

<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1103.5235>

## Width-one conjugation

The exact matrix dictionary in
`M1_COSET_STRATEGY_SOL.md:88-106` is
\[
 A_\lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda^{-1/2},\lambda^{1/2}),\quad
 A_\lambda E A_\lambda^{-1}=Q_\lambda,\quad
 A_\lambda T_\lambda A_\lambda^{-1}=S,
\]
with \(E=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and
\(Q_\lambda=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1/\lambda\\\lambda&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
As a Mobius map, \(A_\lambda:z\mapsto z/\lambda\), so it fixes \(\infty\),
sends the primitive width-\(\lambda\) translation to the primitive unit
translation, and sends \(\operatorname{Stab}(\infty)=\langle T_\lambda\rangle\)
to \(\langle S\rangle\).  The theta endpoint \(\lambda=2\) is covered at the
chosen cusp even though the theta quotient has a second cusp.

## Correct counting object and bound

Use open horoballs.  If
\(\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) has
\(c\neq0\), then \(\gamma H_\infty\) is an open Euclidean disk tangent to
the real line with radius
\[
 R_\gamma=\frac{1}{2c^2}.
\]
Images \(\gamma H_\infty\) are indexed by right cosets \(\Gamma/\Gamma_\infty\)
and are pairwise disjoint by precise invariance.  Quotienting the ambient
plane by left \(\Gamma_\infty\) gives the cylinder \(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\);
its disjoint disk-orbits are indexed by double cosets
\(\Gamma_\infty\backslash\Gamma/\Gamma_\infty\).  Thus, with multiplicity,
\[
 A_\Gamma(X):=\#\{[\gamma]\in\Gamma_\infty\backslash\Gamma/\Gamma_\infty:
                 0<|c_\gamma|\le X\},
\]
choose \(r=(2X^2)^{-1}\).  Every disk with \(|c|\le X\) has a cross-section
arc at height \(r\) of length
\[
 2\sqrt{2R_\gamma r-r^2}\ \geq\ 2r=X^{-2}.
\]
The arcs are disjoint on a circle of circumference one, so
\[
 A_\Gamma(X)\le X^2\qquad(X\ge1).
\]
There is no hidden wraparound factor: Shimizu gives \(R_\gamma\le1/2\), and
the cross-section has length at most \(2R_\gamma\le1\), so projection modulo
one preserves its arc length (including an arc that crosses the chosen seam).
Since \(A_\Gamma(X)\) is integral, one may sharpen the display to
\(A_\Gamma(X)\le\lfloor X^2\rfloor\), but the constant is **one**, not two.
This is a cumulative **double-coset** count.  A one-sided coset set is
infinite at fixed cutoff because left/right powers of the infinite parabolic
stabilizer preserve \(|c|\); only after the cylinder quotient (or an
equivalent one-period choice of representatives) is the finite count obtained.
Open interiors are disjoint; closed horoballs may be tangent, so “closed
disjoint” is not the claim.

The PSL convention is load-bearing.  The note fixes the sign so \(c>0\)
(`M2_G1G2_CLOSURE_SOL.md:139-145`), and the enumerator does the same at
`law_probes/r1_coset_enum.py:104-109`.  If one instead counted SL matrix
double cosets by \(\langle S\rangle\), then \(\gamma\) and \(-\gamma\) could be
counted separately although they define the same horoball; that convention
can introduce the apparent factor two.  It is not the Hejhal/PSL convention
used here.

For \(p=2\sigma>2\), let \(A(t)\) be the right-continuous count using
\(0<|c|\le t\).  For \(Y>X\), Stieltjes summation gives exactly
\[
 \sum_{X<|c_\gamma|\le Y}|c_\gamma|^{-p}
 =Y^{-p}A(Y)-X^{-p}A(X)
   +p\int_X^Y A(t)t^{-p-1}\,dt.
\]
Thus the \(\le X\) convention in \(A\) and the strict \(>X\) tail match; an
atom at \(X\) is canceled by the \(-X^{-p}A(X)\) term.  Now
\(Y^{-p}A(Y)\le Y^{2-p}\to0\), the \(X\)-boundary is non-positive, and
\[
 \sum_{|c_\gamma|>X}|c_\gamma|^{-2\sigma}
 \le p\int_X^\infty t^{1-p}\,dt
 =\frac{p}{p-2}X^{2-p}
 =\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1}X^{2-2\sigma}.
\]
This is valid for \(\sigma>1\) and \(X\ge1\); no integer-grid or floor
assumption on the real \(c\)-spectrum is used.
At \(X=1\) this is a strict-tail bound; adding the possible \(|c|=1\) atom,
with \(A_\Gamma(1)\le1\), gives full-series ceilings 12 at \(\sigma=1.1\)
and 6 at \(\sigma=1.25\).

Uniformity in \(N\) is genuine after conjugation: the only numerical inputs
are the unit cylinder circumference and Shimizu's unit lower bound.  Both are
independent of \(N\).  Pohl supplies discreteness and the primitive
\(T_\lambda\) cusp stabilizer for each group, while the exact conjugation at
`M1_COSET_STRATEGY_SOL.md:88-106` makes that stabilizer \(\langle S\rangle\).
The theta group's second cusp is irrelevant because the argument is local to
the selected cusp at infinity.
