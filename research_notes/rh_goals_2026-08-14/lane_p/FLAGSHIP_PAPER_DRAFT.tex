\begin{abstract}
Let $G_5$ be the Hecke triangle group with $\lambda_5 = 2\cos(\pi/5)$
(the golden ratio), $X_5 = G_5\backslash\HH$ the associated
non-arithmetic finite-area hyperbolic orbifold, and $Z_S(s)$ its
Selberg zeta function. We give a rigorous, computer-assisted proof that
$Z_S$ has a zero $s^*$ with
$|\Re(s^*) - 0.4538951800749447| \le 10^{-6}$ and
$|\Im(s^*) - 5.7635372417301305| \le 10^{-6}$.
In particular $\Re(s^*) \le 0.4538962 < 1/2$, so $s^*$ lies strictly
off the critical line with essential gap
$\delta := 1/2 - \Re(s^*) \ge 0.0461038$; by the standard spectral
interpretation of Selberg zeros, $s^*$ is a scattering resonance of
$X_5$. A theorem of Selberg, published by Hejhal
\cite[Thm~7.11, Cor.~7.12]{HejhalLNM1001} and restated by
Garbin--Jorgenson \cite{GarbinJorgenson18}, already asserts that
arbitrarily small rectangles adjacent to the critical line contain
zeros and poles of the Hecke scattering determinant $\varphi_N$ for all
sufficiently large $N$; that statement is asymptotic and ineffective in
$N$, and names no individual group. What is new here is the
localization at a \emph{specific} non-arithmetic surface, with an
explicit certified box, explicit constants, and machine-checkable
receipts. The proof
combines interval-certified contour winding of a Fredholm determinant
at truncation level $N=160$ (python-flint Arb/Acb ball arithmetic at
384 bits), a machine-verified finite-section identity and supporting
functional-analytic lemmas in Lean, the determinant comparison bounds
of Gohberg--Krein/Simon, the Mayer--M\"uller--Seeger factorization
$Z_S = \det(1-L_{s,+})\det(1-L_{s,-})/\det(1-K_s)$, and a certified
nonvanishing gate on the $K_s$ divisor. As a corollary, the non-real
strip zeros of $Z_{G_5}$ are not all contained in the critical line;
no further distributional claim is made.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Background and positioning}

For a finite-area hyperbolic surface or orbifold $X = \Gamma\backslash\HH$,
the zeros of the Selberg zeta function $Z_S(s)$ in the strip
$0 < \Re(s) < 1$ encode both the discrete spectrum of the Laplacian
(zeros on $\Re(s) = 1/2$ or on the real segment) and the poles of the
meromorphically continued scattering matrix, i.e.\ the
\emph{resonances} of $X$ (zeros off both). For arithmetic $\Gamma$
(e.g.\ the modular group and its congruence covers), the location of
many zeros is governed by arithmetic objects --- the nontrivial factor
$\zeta(2s)$ pins scattering poles to the line $\Re(s) = 1/4$ --- and the
classical Selberg theory applies. For non-arithmetic $\Gamma$ no such
structure is available, and the Phillips--Sarnak deformation theory
\cite{PhillipsSarnak85,PhillipsSarnak85b} suggests that cusp forms
generically dissolve into resonances, placing zeros of $Z_S$ off the
critical line $\Re(s) = 1/2$.

\subsection{Prior art: the Selberg--Hejhal large-$N$ theorem}
\label{sec:priorart}

That off-line scattering data occur in the Hecke triangle family is not
new, and we state the antecedent before positioning our own result.
Let $\varphi_N(s)$ denote the scalar scattering determinant of $G_N$.
Hejhal \cite[Thm~7.11 and Cor.~7.12]{HejhalLNM1001}, attributing the
result to Selberg (ending remarks of Selberg's G\"ottingen lectures,
part~2), proves: given $t_0 \in \mathbb{R}$ and $0 < \delta < 1$, the
rectangle $[\tfrac12, \tfrac12+\delta]\times[t_0-\delta, t_0+\delta]$
contains zeros of $\varphi_N(s)$, and the reflected rectangle
$[\tfrac12-\delta, \tfrac12]\times[t_0-\delta, t_0+\delta]$ contains
poles of $\varphi_N(s)$, for all sufficiently large $N$. The statement
is reproduced verbatim, and set in the framework of elliptic
degeneration $G_N \to G_\infty$, by Garbin--Jorgenson
\cite[p.~162]{GarbinJorgenson18}, who additionally quantify the
accumulation of scattering poles
\cite[Thm~5.7, Rmk~5.8]{GarbinJorgenson18}. Since $\varphi_N$ is
unitary on the regular part of $\Re(s) = 1/2$, these zeros and poles
are genuinely off the critical line. The qualitative existence of
off-line scattering data in this family is therefore a theorem of long
standing.

What that theorem does not supply is exactly what a certificate
supplies. It is asymptotic in $N$: no threshold $N_0$ is extracted, so
it settles no individual group; it names no surface, produces no box,
no constants, and no verifiable receipts; and arithmeticity plays no
role in its mechanism. Accordingly we position the present paper as
follows. (a) Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is, to our knowledge
\cite{NoveltyRescout}, the first rigorous localization of an off-line
resonance at a \emph{specific} non-arithmetic finite-area hyperbolic
surface, with an explicit box, an explicit gap
$\delta \ge 0.0461038$, and explicit certified margins. (b) Every
numerical input is a machine-checkable interval certificate and several
functional-analytic joints are formally verified in Lean
(\S\ref{sec:lean}). (c) \S\ref{sec:mechanism} derives the mechanism on
the arithmetic side --- $\zeta(2s)$ appearing as the scattering
determinant of the associated Fricke group --- which is the ingredient
that makes the arithmetic/non-arithmetic contrast a mechanism rather
than a coincidence of data. (d) The natural continuation is an
\emph{effective} version: an explicit threshold $Q_0$ beyond which the
Selberg--Hejhal conclusion holds, together with certified verification
below it. The literature does not currently contain such a threshold,
and none is claimed here.

For the Hecke triangle groups $G_q$, the transfer-operator approach of
Mayer and its development by M\"uhlenbruch, Str\"omberg, M\"oller--Pohl
and others (see \S\ref{sec:prelim}) provides a Fredholm-determinant
realization of $Z_S$ amenable to computation \cite{MMS,MoellerPohl13}.
High-precision \emph{numerical} studies of Selberg zeta zeros and
resonances in this and related settings exist --- notably Fr\k{a}czek's
transfer-operator computations \cite{FraczekBook17}, the
Bruggeman--Fr\k{a}czek--Mayer study of the zeros of $Z_S$ for
$\Gamma_0(4)$ \cite{BFM13}, and the Lagrange--Chebyshev computations of
Bandtlow--Pohl--Schick--Wei{\ss}e for infinite-area Schottky surfaces
\cite{BandtlowPohlSchickWeisse21}
--- but these computations, however accurate, do not carry rigorous
error enclosures: they are validated numerics, not certificates. On the
rigorous side, validated (interval/pseudospectral) enclosure methods
for transfer-operator resonances have been implemented for uniformly
expanding circle maps \cite{Pseudospectral25}, a different system class
that does not include hyperbolic surfaces; and high-precision
computations of Hausdorff dimensions for Hecke triangle groups
\cite{HausdorffDim25} explicitly use asymptotic error bounds rather than
interval certification. The Fredholm-determinant framework of
M\"oller--Pohl \cite{MoellerPohl13} supplies the analytic setting but
makes no claim of localizing individual non-arithmetic Hecke
resonances; the contemporary strict
transfer-operator framework of Pohl--Wabnitz \cite{PohlWabnitz26} is
theoretical and does not claim computational zero localization.

The present paper closes one instance of this gap with full rigor, in
the computer-assisted sense: every numerical input is an interval
certificate with explicit radii, and every reduction step is either a
short self-contained paper proof, a cited published theorem, or a
Lean-checked formal lemma.

\subsection{Main result}

\begin{theorem}[Main theorem, computer-assisted]
\label{thm:main}
Let $G_5$ be the Hecke triangle group with
$\lambda_5 = 2\cos(\pi/5)$, $X_5 = G_5\backslash\HH$, and $Z_S(s)$ the
Selberg zeta function of $X_5$, meromorphically continued to $\CC$.
Then $Z_S$ has a zero $s^*$ with
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:box}
|\Re(s^*) - 0.4538951800749447| \le 10^{-6},
\qquad
|\Im(s^*) - 5.7635372417301305| \le 10^{-6}.
\end{equation}
In particular $\Re(s^*) \le 0.4538962 < 1/2$, so $s^*$ lies off the
critical line with essential gap
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gap}
\delta := \tfrac12 - \Re(s^*) \ge 0.0461038.
\end{equation}
Since $\Im(s^*) \approx 5.76 \neq 0$, $s^*$ is neither a
small-eigenvalue parameter (those are real) nor a tempered-eigenvalue
zero (those lie on $\Re(s) = 1/2$); by the standard spectral
interpretation of finite-area Selberg zeros in the strip
$0 < \Re(s) < 1/2$ (Hejhal \cite{HejhalLNM1001}, Iwaniec
\cite{IwaniecGSM53}, Borthwick \cite{BorthwickBook}), $s^*$ is a
scattering \emph{resonance} of $X_5$.
\end{theorem}

To our knowledge Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is the first rigorous
localization of an off-line resonance at a specific non-arithmetic
finite-area hyperbolic surface \cite{NoveltyRescout}; the qualitative
large-$N$ antecedent of Selberg--Hejhal
\cite[Thm~7.11, Cor.~7.12]{HejhalLNM1001}, \cite{GarbinJorgenson18} is
recalled in \S\ref{sec:priorart}, and the present statement is
independent of it (that theorem yields no threshold, and $q = 5$ is the
smallest non-arithmetic member). The proof is an eight-link
chain (\S\ref{sec:chain}) mixing machine-certified interval receipts,
Lean-proved joints, cited published theorems, and short self-contained
arguments; the dependency classes are made explicit in
\S\ref{sec:deps} and the certificates are tabulated in
\S\ref{sec:certs}.

\subsection*{Round-down discipline}
All margins quoted in this paper are \emph{lower bounds rounded down}
from the certified interval receipts (e.g.\ the minimum exclusion
margin is quoted as $3.43786\times 10^{-8}$ from the receipt ball
$3.4378649\ldots\times 10^{-8}$). No certified quantity is ever rounded
in the direction that would strengthen a claim.

\section{Preliminaries, conventions, and dependency classes}
\label{sec:prelim}
\label{sec:deps}

\subsection{The Hecke orbifold and the transfer operator}

Let $G_q = \langle S, T_q \rangle$, $S z = -1/z$,
$T_q z = z + \lambda_q$, $\lambda_q = 2\cos(\pi/q)$. For $q = 5$,
$\lambda_5 = (1+\sqrt5)/2$ and $G_5$ is non-arithmetic (Hecke triangle
groups are arithmetic exactly for $q \in \{3,4,6\}$). The Mayer
transfer operator, in the reduced three-component form used by
Mayer--M\"uller--Seeger (MMS) \cite{MMS}, acts on vector-valued
functions on three discs $D_j = D(c_j, a_j h_j)$; we work with the
$P$-symmetric (continued-fraction reflection) sector $L_{s,+}$ of
\cite[eq.~(34)]{MMS} at $q = 5$, sector sign $+1$, with radius factors
exactly $3.14$, $2.27$, $1.70$ and head split $4$, as bound to the
sha-pinned certifying wrapper \cite{R3bReceipt}.

\begin{remark}[MMS source caveat]
\label{rem:mmsq}
The heading above \cite[eq.~(34)]{MMS} prints ``$q = 2h_q + 3 > 5$''
while \cite[Lemma~4.2]{MMS} states $q \ge 5$; the $q = 5$
identification rests on the general incidence formula, not on the
displayed heading. This footnote is mandatory per the audit trail.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Sector honesty]
$L_{s,+}$ is the MMS $P$-symmetric sector, \emph{not} the geometric
even/odd Maass sector. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} deliberately attaches no
parity label to $s^*$ --- only existence and location are claimed.
\end{remark}

Two realizations of the same block system are used:
$L_s^{H}$ on $H = \bigoplus_j H^2(D_j)$ (Hardy spaces, the certified
realization) and $L_s^{B}$ on $B = \bigoplus_j B(D_j)$ (disc algebras,
the MMS setting, nuclear of order zero \cite[Thm~4.10]{MMS}).

\section{The proof chain}
\label{sec:chain}

We state the eight links; each link names its dependency class and its
in-repo receipt. The full technical content is in the cited reports;
here we give the logical skeleton and the certified constants.

\subsection*{Link 1: Finite winding and closed-contour exclusion
\emph{[machine-certified]}}

At truncation level $N = 160$ (per component; $\kappa = 3$), the finite
determinant $\det(I - P_N L_{s,+} P_N)$ has certified argument winding
$1$ around the boundary of the box
\[
\mathrm{Box} = \{ s : |\Re(s) - 0.4538951800749447| \le 10^{-6},\
|\Im(s) - 5.7635372417301305| \le 10^{-6} \}.
\]
The boundary is covered by $284$ closed Acb subarcs ($71$ per edge;
adaptive split budget depth $8$, achieved max depth $1$; census $100$
depth-$0$ plus $184$ depth-$1$). On every subarc, the finite Taylor
enclosure \emph{and} the $F_R$-inflated enclosure exclude $0$; the
minimum certified margin (finite lower bound minus $F_R$) is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:margin}
\ge 3.43786\times 10^{-8}
\quad\text{(lower bound, rounded down from the receipt ball
$3.4378649\ldots\times 10^{-8}$),}
\end{equation}
and the per-subarc self-consistency factor satisfies
$rH \le 0.359 < 1$. Receipt: \cite{R3bReceipt} (384-bit Arb/Acb;
\texttt{all\_theorem\_gates\_pass = True}; immutable input shas
verified). Independent re-checks confirmed all $284$ records' four
gates, the $71\times 4$ census, and recomputed the minimum margin from
raw fields.

\begin{remark}[Serialization caveat]
The printed per-arc argument increments reconstruct the winding $1$
only to $\sim 10^{-99}$, not to the receipt ball's full width
$7.81\times 10^{-114}$; reproducing the tighter ball requires re-running
the sha-pinned code, not the serialized deltas alone.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Link 2: Finite section equals $\det_H(I - L P_N)$
\emph{[paper-proof + Lean]}}

The computed matrix determinant equals the Hilbert-space determinant
$\detH(I - L P_N)$ by the finite-rank restriction identity followed by
Sylvester's determinant identity (Steps 1--2 of \cite{R1Restatement};
Steps 3--4 of that report are superseded --- its step-3 $F_R$ used a
voided envelope and its geometric-tail claim was disowned; the
operative $F_R$ and endpoint bounds are those of \cite{R3bReport}).
The restriction identity is machine-proved in Lean as
\texttt{det\_one\_sub\_proj\_mul\_proj} \cite{LeanV18}, sorry-free and
axiom-clean, with one documented statement adjustment (the Euclidean
column written via \texttt{WithLp.toLp}, same $L^2$ norm).

\subsection*{Link 3: Determinant comparison
\emph{[paper-proof + machine constants + Lean joints]}}

By the Gohberg--Krein/Simon trace-ideal perturbation bound (in the form
of \cite{SimonTraceIdeals}),
\[
\bigl| \det(I - L) - \det(I - L P_N) \bigr|
\;\le\;
T_{\mathrm{tail}}(N)\cdot
\exp\bigl(1 + \norm{L}_1 + \norm{L P_N}_1\bigr).
\]
The machine-certified constants \cite{R3bReceipt} are: both endpoint
trace norms $\norm{L}_1, \norm{L P_N}_1 \le 17.2911968$ (computed-row
column $2$-norms plus enlarged-disc output-tail corrections plus
$T_{\mathrm{tail}}(160)$); $T_{\mathrm{tail}}(160) = 6.26786\times
10^{-22}$ (immutable R2 receipt \cite{R2Receipt}, all $11$ block
families, center offsets kept, Hurwitz-closed $m=0$ term); and the
resulting inflation constant
\[
F_R(160) \;=\; T_{\mathrm{tail}}(160)\,
e^{\,1 + 2\cdot 17.2911968\ldots} \;=\; 1.77974\times 10^{-6}.
\]
Supporting Lean joints \cite{LeanV18,LeanV19} (all axiom-clean):
\texttt{trace\_unitary\_le\_sum\_column\_norms},
\texttt{l2\_le\_card\_mul\_sup\_sq},
\texttt{coeff\_bound\_of\_uniform}, \texttt{geom\_tail\_le}.

\subsection*{Link 4: A zero of the true determinant in the box
\emph{[argument principle]}}

Since every $F_R$-inflated tube excludes $0$, the straight-line
homotopy $\det(I - L P_N) \to \det(I - L)$ never vanishes on
$\partial\mathrm{Box}$; hence the winding of $\det(I - L_{s,+})$ around
$\partial\mathrm{Box}$ is $1$, and $\det(I - L_{s,+})$ has exactly one
zero $s^*$ (counted with multiplicity) in $\mathrm{Box}$.

\subsection*{Link 4b: Hilbert-to-Banach transport
\emph{[paper-proof + machine + independently reviewed]}}

On the open connected region
$\Omega^* = \{\Re s > 1/2\} \cup \{\Re s > 0,\ \Im s > 1\}$, which
contains $\mathrm{Box}$,
\[
\detH(1 - L_{s,+}) = \detB(1 - L_{s,+}^{\mathrm{MMS}}).
\]
The proof \cite{R5Report} proceeds by: (i) smoothing via the enlarged
discs $D_i^{0.1} = D(c_i, R_i + 0.1)$, certified to contract with
$\hat\rho \le 0.9484 < 1$ and minimum pole/cut clearance
$\ge 1.0023$ on all eleven block families (receipt \cite{E1Receipt},
independently reproduced by two adversarial reviews); (ii) Jordan-chain
equality of the nonzero spectra with algebraic multiplicities;
(iii) spectral canonical determinants on both sides (Simon
\cite[Thm~4.2]{Simon77} on $H$; Grothendieck \cite[Thm~8]{Grothendieck52}
on $B$, since $L_s^{B}$ is nuclear of order zero, hence $2/3$-nuclear,
by \cite[Thm~4.10]{MMS}); and (iv) locally uniform trace-class
holomorphy on $\Omega^*$ plus the identity theorem.
(Bandtlow--Jenkinson \cite{BandtlowJenkinson08} prove
space-independence of the dynamical determinant for single-domain
systems including $B(D)$ and $H^2(D)$ and are cited as corroboration
only, in the standard transfer-operator usage of Ruelle
\cite{Ruelle76}; the proof above does not rely on extending their
hypotheses to the graph-directed setting.)
Hence the zero of
Link~4 is a zero of the MMS Banach determinant consumed by MMS
Theorem~6.4.

\subsection*{Link 5: No divisor cancellation \emph{[machine-certified, closed]}}

$\det(1 - K_s) \neq 0$ on $\mathrm{Box}$: the zero set of
$\det(1 - K_s)$ is the exact lattice $s = -n + i\pi k / a_q$, all with
$\Re(s) \le 0$ \cite{KSGate,KSCrosscheck,LeanV17}, while $\mathrm{Box}$
sits at $\Re \approx 0.454 > 0$. (Precision note: the recorded $G_5$
distance $0.4551002$ is center-to-lattice, not box-to-lattice; the box
half-diagonal $\sqrt2\cdot 10^{-6}$ is five orders of magnitude below
it, so nonvanishing on the closed box follows.)

\subsection*{Link 6: Selberg zeta factorization \emph{[cited]}}

MMS Theorem~6.4 \cite{MMS} gives
\[
Z_S(s) \;=\;
\frac{\det(1 - L_{s,+})\,\det(1 - L_{s,-})}{\det(1 - K_s)}.
\]
The factor $\det(1 - L_{s,-})$ is analytic near $\mathrm{Box}$: the MMS
determinants are meromorphic with poles only at real points
$s = (1-k)/2$ \cite[Thm~4.10]{MMS}, and $\mathrm{Box}$ has
$\Im \approx 5.76$. With Link~5, $Z_S(s^*) = 0$ with multiplicity at
least that of the $+$ factor.

\subsection*{Link 7: Resonance interpretation \emph{[cited, standard]}}

For finite-area surfaces, zeros of $Z_S$ in $0 < \Re(s) < 1/2$ off the
real axis are resonances (scattering poles of the meromorphically
continued resolvent/scattering matrix); discrete-spectrum zeros lie on
$\Re(s) = 1/2$ or on the real segment
\cite{HejhalLNM1001,IwaniecGSM53,BorthwickBook}. Since
$\Im(s^*) \approx 5.76 \neq 0$, the discrete alternatives are excluded.
This completes the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. \qed

\section{Corollary: the non-real strip zeros are not all on the
critical line}
\label{sec:cor}

Write
\[
\Lambda := \{ s \in \CC : Z_S(s) = 0 \},\quad
\Lambda^\circ := \Lambda \cap \{ 0 < \Re(s) < 1,\ \Im(s) \neq 0 \},\quad
L := \{ \Re(s) = \tfrac12 \}.
\]
The restriction to $\Lambda^\circ$ is bookkeeping, not weakness:
real-axis zeros (trivial zeros and small-eigenvalue parameters) lie off
$L$ for \emph{every} finite-area surface and carry no information.

\begin{corollary}[weak vertical-line form]
\label{cor:line}
There exists $s^* \in \Lambda^\circ$ satisfying \eqref{eq:box}, with
$\Re(s^*) \le 0.4538962$ and gap $\delta \ge 0.0461038$. Hence
$\Lambda^\circ \not\subset L$: the non-real strip zeros of $Z_{G_5}$
are not all on the critical line, and the deviation is quantitative.
Equivalently, no vertical line $V_c = \{\Re(s) = c\}$ with $c = 1/2$
contains $\Lambda^\circ$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Theorem~\ref{thm:main}:
$\Re(s^*) \le 0.4538951800749447 + 10^{-6} = 0.4538962 < 1/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope limits --- what is \emph{not} claimed]
\label{rem:scope}
(i) Corollary~\ref{cor:line} refutes the single line $\Re(s) = 1/2$
only; one certified pin cannot exclude $\Lambda^\circ \subset
V_{0.45390}$ for some other line, nor ``finitely many vertical lines''
(excluding one line in general needs two certified pins at distinct
real parts; that remains open). (ii) No claim about all or most zeros,
no density or counting statement, and no assertion that
$\delta \ge 0.0461038$ is the maximal gap. (iii) Nothing is asserted
for $G_q$, $q \neq 5$. (iv) No ``arithmetic $\Leftrightarrow$ critical
line'' law: the contrast with the arithmetic members $q = 3, 4, 6$
(whose strip zeros are pinned by the $\zeta(2s)$ factor) is descriptive
of data, within a single commensurability class, and must not be read
as a proved equivalence or a conjecture with evidential weight.
Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} derives \emph{why} the $\zeta(2s)$ factor is
present for $q = 4, 6$ --- it is the scattering determinant of the
corresponding Fricke group --- but a mechanism on one side of a contrast
is not an equivalence, and \S\ref{sec:mechanism} adds no claim to
this list.
\end{remark}

\section{The arithmetic mechanism: $\zeta(2s)$ as an Eisenstein
scattering determinant}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Corollary~\ref{cor:line} are statements about
$G_5$ alone. This section explains, for the \emph{arithmetic} members
$q = 4$ and $q = 6$, \emph{why} their strip zeros are pinned by
$\zeta(2s)$: the factor is the scattering determinant of the associated
Fricke group, obtained from the constant term of its Eisenstein series.
The material of this section is a derivation with two cited inputs and
some non-rigorous numerical confirmations; its dependency status is
stated explicitly throughout, and none of it is used in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. Receipt: \cite{M1F,M1D,M1E}.

\subsection{The Fricke identification and the single cusp}

Let $p$ be prime, $W_p z = -1/(pz)$ the normalized Fricke involution,
and $\Gamma_0^+(p) = \Gamma_0(p) \cup \Gamma_0(p)W_p$. For
$(q,p) = (4,2)$ and $(6,3)$ one has $\lambda_q = \sqrt{p}$, and the
explicit conjugator $D = \operatorname{diag}(p^{1/4}, p^{-1/4})$
satisfies $D^{-1}T_{\lambda_q}D = T$ and $D^{-1}SD = W_p$, whence
$D^{-1}G_qD = \langle W_p, T\rangle = \Gamma_0^+(p)$
\cite[\S1.4]{M1F} (the identification itself is classical
\cite{Takeuchi77}). Two features of this conjugator are load bearing.
First, $W_p$ interchanges the two cusps $\infty$ and $0$ of
$\Gamma_0(p)$, so $\Gamma_0^+(p)$ has exactly \emph{one} cusp, of
unchanged width \cite[\S1.2--1.3]{M1F}. Second, $D$ \emph{is} the cusp
scaling matrix of $G_q$ at $\infty$, so the scattering function
transports with no factor $\lambda^{2s-1}$:
$\varphi_{G_q} = \varphi_{\Gamma_0^+(p)}$ exactly
\cite[eq.~(1.4)]{M1F}. Two independent volume computations
($\operatorname{vol} = \pi(1-2/q)$ from the $(2,q,\infty)$ triangle,
and $(\pi/6)(p+1)$ from the index) agree for both $q$
\cite[\S1.5]{M1F}.

\subsection{The scattering determinant in closed form}

Writing $\Gamma' = \Gamma_0(p)$, a coset decomposition
$\Gamma'_\infty\backslash\Gamma_0^+(p) =
(\Gamma'_\infty\backslash\Gamma') \sqcup
(\Gamma'_\infty\backslash\Gamma'W_p)$ --- an argument with no analytic
input --- gives the Eisenstein identities
$E^+ = E_\infty + E_0$ and $E^\chi = E_\infty - E_0$, where $\chi$ is
the nontrivial character of $\Gamma_0^+(p)/\Gamma_0(p)$
\cite[\S2.2--2.3]{M1F}. Taking constant terms, the (one-cusp)
scattering functions are $\varphi^{\pm} = \varphi_{\infty\infty} \pm
\varphi_{\infty 0}$, i.e.\ exactly the eigenvalue decomposition of the
$2\times 2$ matrix $\Phi(s)$ of $\Gamma_0(p)$ on the $W_p$-symmetric and
antisymmetric vectors. Evaluating the allowed-moduli constant-term
formula for the Fourier expansion of Eisenstein series (the single
analytic import, taken at content level from Iwaniec
\cite{IwaniecGSM53} and Hejhal \cite{HejhalLNM1001}; the equation
numbering is deliberately not asserted here, see
Remark~\ref{rem:mechscope}) against the Euler product of
$\zeta(2s-1)/\zeta(2s)$, and doing the resulting rational algebra in
$X = p^s$, yields \cite[\S3--4]{M1F}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:phipm}
\varphi^+_p(s) = g(s)\,\frac{1 + p^{1-s}}{1+p^s},
\qquad
\varphi^-_p(s) = g(s)\,\frac{p^{1-s}-1}{p^s-1},
\qquad
g(s) := \frac{\Lambda(2s-1)}{\Lambda(2s)},
\end{equation}
with $\Lambda(w) = \pi^{-w/2}\Gamma(w/2)\zeta(w)$, so that
$g(s) = \sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(s-\tfrac12)\zeta(2s-1)/
(\Gamma(s)\zeta(2s))$. A second derivation, applying the same
constant-term formula directly to $\Gamma_0^+(p)$ and counting its own
allowed moduli, reproduces \eqref{eq:phipm} term by term without ever
mentioning the $\Gamma_0(p)$ scattering matrix
\cite[\S3.5]{M1F}. Three self-checks pass exactly: the functional
equation $\varphi^{\pm}(s)\varphi^{\pm}(1-s) = 1$, which is now a
one-line consequence of $\Lambda(w) = \Lambda(1-w)$ rather than a
numerical observation; the residue
$\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\varphi^+_p = 6/(\pi(p+1)) =
1/\operatorname{vol}(\Gamma_0^+(p)\backslash\HH)$, the Selberg
normalization for a one-cusp group; and the specialization to $p=2,3$
\cite[\S4.1--4.4]{M1F}.

\subsection{Consequence: the $\zeta(2s)$ pin, and a second divisor}

Equation~\eqref{eq:phipm} exhibits the whole divisor of $g$. Its poles
include $s = \rho/2$ for every nontrivial zero $\rho$ of $\zeta$, all on
$\Re(s) = 1/4$; the elementary factors are finite and nonzero there, so
these poles survive in both sectors. Under the classical transport of
scattering poles into the divisor of $Z_S$ --- ``$Z$ has a zero at
$\beta$ of the same order as the zero of $\det\Phi$ at $1-\bar\beta$''
for $\Re\beta < 1/2$, quoted from Bruggeman--Fr\k{a}czek--Mayer
\cite{BFM13} and independently reachable from the closed-form Selberg
functional equation of Teo \cite{Teo20} --- this says: \emph{for the
arithmetic Hecke triangle groups $G_4$ and $G_6$, the nontrivial zeros
of $\zeta$ enter the Selberg divisor along the vertical line
$\Re(s) = 1/4$, and they do so because the surface has one cusp whose
Eisenstein constant term is \eqref{eq:phipm}} \cite[\S5]{M1F},
\cite{LawU3}.

The same closed form predicts a \emph{second}, non-Riemann divisor. The
elementary factors have poles exactly at
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:elemdiv}
\text{trivial sector: } 1 + p^s = 0
\iff s = \frac{i(2k+1)\pi}{\log p};
\qquad
\text{$\chi$ sector: } p^s = 1
\iff s = \frac{2ik\pi}{\log p},\ k \ne 0,
\end{equation}
all on $\Re(s) = 0$; the exclusion $k \ne 0$ in the $\chi$ sector is
forced by an exact zero/pole cancellation between $g$ and the elementary
factor at $s = 0$, not stipulated \cite[\S5.3]{M1F}. These points are
neither Riemann zeros nor Maass parameters, so they are a falsifiable
signature of the derivation.

\subsection{Numerical confirmation, and its exact status}

The eight points of \eqref{eq:elemdiv} with $|k| \le 2$ --- four at
$p=2$ ($\pi/\log 2 = 4.532360\ldots$) and four at $p=3$
($\pi/\log 3 = 2.859600\ldots$) --- were evaluated against the existing
unmodified even-$q$ transfer-operator builder at $N = 60$. All eight
vanish, in \emph{exactly} the sector \eqref{eq:elemdiv} assigns and not
the other, with the off-sector determinant of order $10^0$--$10^1$ at
the same point, against nine nearby controls of order $10^{-1}$--$10^1$
on both determinants, and stably under $N = 40 \to 60$: at $q=4$ the
four magnitudes are $5.67\times 10^{-30}$, $7.68\times 10^{-27}$
(trivial sector) and $4.62\times 10^{-30}$, $7.12\times 10^{-21}$
($\chi$ sector) \cite[\S6.2]{M1D}; at $q=6$ they are
$1.01\times 10^{-15}$, $4.27\times 10^{-14}$ and
$5.37\times 10^{-15}$, $1.09\times 10^{-13}$ \cite[\S4.2]{M1E}. In the
same runs the Riemann pins $s = \rho_j/2$ on $\Re(s) = 1/4$ vanish in
both Fricke sectors, as \eqref{eq:phipm} requires of a common factor
$g$, while the opposite MMS sector stays of order one
\cite[\S6.1]{M1D}, \cite[\S4.1]{M1E}.

\begin{remark}[Status of these numerics --- \textbf{non-rigorous}]
\label{rem:mechnum}
Every number in this subsection is a floating \emph{midpoint} of an Arb
ball at finite truncation. \textbf{No} ball enclosure, \textbf{no}
argument-principle isolation and \textbf{no} dimension-tail certificate
is claimed for any of them; they are validated numerics of the same
kind this paper distinguishes from certificates in
\S\ref{sec:prelim}. A separate attempt to promote the eight points to
interval-certified winding certificates returned
\textbf{$0/8$ rigorously certified} \cite{M1G}: four trivial-sector
runs produced sampled windings equal to $1$, but through a routine that
uses a heuristic dimension-tail inflation rather than the theorem-grade
uniform bound of Link~3, and the four $\chi$-sector determinants have no
existing winding entry point at all. \emph{No resonance of $G_4$ or
$G_6$ is certified in this paper.} The only certified zero here is the
$G_5$ zero of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{What the mechanism does and does not add to the
arithmetic/non-arithmetic contrast}

Alongside the derivation, nine interval evaluations record the
\emph{absence} of the $\zeta(2s)$ pin on non-arithmetic members: at
$s = \rho_j/2$ for $j = 1,2,3$ the relevant transfer-operator
determinant is bounded away from zero for $q = 5, 8, 10$, with
certified lower bounds between $4.91\times 10^{-1}$ and
$1.28\times 10^{1}$, while the arithmetic control $q = 4$ at
$s = \rho_1/2$ is consistent with zero (lower bound $0$, midpoint
$4.58\times 10^{-16}$) \cite{M2}. These witnesses carry the label
\emph{certified-modulo-tail-heuristic}: the finite-truncation and
rounding enclosures are Arb ball arithmetic, but the dimension-tail
component of the radius is the existing heuristic extrapolation, and
three tested points are pointwise evidence, not a no-factor theorem.

\begin{remark}[Scope of \S\ref{sec:mechanism}]
\label{rem:mechscope}
The V1 ruling of Remark~\ref{rem:scope} continues to bind, and this
section does not weaken it. In particular: (i) \S\ref{sec:mechanism}
concerns $q = 4, 6$ only, which lie in a single commensurability class,
so it supplies a \emph{mechanism} for the arithmetic side, not a second
data point and not a law; there is still \textbf{no} claim that
arithmeticity is equivalent to critical-line confinement, and
\textbf{no} family claim for $G_q$, $q \ne 5$. The honest statement of
the contrast is: on the arithmetic side the pinning is explained by a
derived closed form together with non-rigorous numerics; on the
non-arithmetic side one point is a theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:main}). (ii) The derivation of \eqref{eq:phipm}
imports one constant-term formula whose \emph{content} is textbook but
whose theorem number we do not assert, and the transport statement is
cited to \cite{BFM13,Teo20}, which in turn attribute it to Hejhal
\cite{HejhalLNM1001}; the primary page check is an open obligation
\cite{LawU3}. (iii) On novelty: an in-repo prior-art scout
\cite{FrickePriorArt} did not locate \eqref{eq:phipm}, nor the divisor
\eqref{eq:elemdiv}, in any accessible source (Friedman
\texttt{math/0702030}, the Jorgenson--Smajlovi\'c--Then moonshine-group
literature). We therefore say only that \emph{we are not aware of} a
published closed form for the Fricke scattering determinant or of these
resonances; two canonical sources --- Hejhal \cite{HejhalLNM1001} and
Huxley's \emph{Scattering matrices for congruence subgroups} (1984) ---
were \textbf{not} accessible to that scout and remain unresolved, and
the prior base rate for such a formula already existing is high. No
priority claim is made for \eqref{eq:phipm} or \eqref{eq:elemdiv}.
\end{remark}
