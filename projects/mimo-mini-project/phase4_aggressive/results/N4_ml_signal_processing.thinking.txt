## Understanding the Pipeline

The pipeline is:
1. Take a sequence indexed by primes (or log-spaced sampling)
2. Apply bias normalization (removing the smooth/expected part, like the prime counting function approximation)
3. Apply MUSIC (Multiple Signal Classification) algorithm — a subspace method for line-spectral estimation
4. Extract "L-zero phases" — the frequencies of the complex exponentials in the residual

The key insight is that the Riemann zeta function zeros appear as frequencies in the prime-counting oscillation. The "signal" is the error term π(x) - Li(x), which is a sum of complex exponentials at frequencies corresponding to the imaginary parts of the zeta zeros.

## What Makes This Pipeline Special?

1. **Log-spaced sampling** — this is crucial because the prime-counting function naturally lives on a log scale
2. **Bias normalization** — removing the dominant smooth trend
3. **MUSIC for subspace estimation** — exploiting sparsity of the spectrum
4. **The spectrum is expected to be sparse** — in the number theory case, there are (conjecturally) infinitely many zeros but they are discrete and structured
