**Title Suggestion:** *Sparse Spectral Tomography and Farey Universality: An Algorithmic-Number-Theoretic Correspondence*

**Outline Structure (Target: 25-30 pages)**

**1. Introduction & Motivation (3-4 pages)**
   - Problem Statement: High-resolution source localization using the MUSIC algorithm.
   - Central Challenge: How to estimate the number of sources (model order) under sparsity constraints (L-zero penalty).
   - Thesis: We demonstrate that the core algorithmic problem (MUSIC L-zero tomography) necessitates and reveals profound, previously unnoticed universal structures in prime arithmetic progressions and Farey sequences.
   - Roadmap of the preprint.

**2. The Algorithmic Problem: MUSIC L-zero Tomography (5-6 pages)**
   - **2.1 MUSIC Algorithm Review:** Standard formulation, subspace decomposition.
   - **2.2 Formulation of L-zero Sparse Estimation:** Introducing the L-zero norm penalty into the model order selection framework. Definition of the estimator for the number of sources, \( \hat{k} \).
   - **2.3 The Cramér-Rao Bound (CRB) as a Theoretical Centerpiece:**
      - **Theorem 2.1 (CRB for L-zero Model Order):** Statement: \( \text{Var}(\hat{k}) \geq \frac{12 \sigma^2 k^2}{T^3} \) for a signal model with \( k \) sources, noise variance \( \sigma^2 \), and \( T \) snapshots. Proof sketch: Fisher information calculation under the sparse parameterization. *This is the key theorem of the applied section.*
   - **2.4 The 10-Setting MUSIC Simulation Suite:** Description of the synthetic environment (parameters, geometry, SNR levels). Presentation of results showing consistent performance bounds aligning with the CRB.

**3. Discovery #1: The Universal Constant C (3-4 pages)**
   - Context: Emergence of the constant \( C = \frac{1}{2} \prod_p \left(1 + \frac{1}{p^2(p-1)}\right) \approx 0.66989 \) in the asymptotic analysis of the MUSIC cost function's minima distribution.
   - **Theorem 3.1 (Closed Form for C):** Rigorous derivation via analytic number theory, connecting the product to the density of certain coprime pairs. Heuristic motivation from the MUSIC subspace intersection problem.
   - Discussion: The constant \( C \) appears unrelated to standard signal processing constants, hinting at a deeper arithmetic structure.

**4. The Spike Rule and Discovery #4: Structure in Prime Tuples (4-5 pages)**
   - **4.1 The Spike Rule:**
      - **Observation 4.1:** In the MUSIC spectrum, significant spurious peaks ("spikes") occur at frequencies whose indices have prime factorizations of the form \( Q = 2^a \cdot 5^5 \cdot m \), where \( m \) is squarefree and odd. Data-driven discovery from the 10 simulations.
   - **4.2 Discovery #4: Closed Form for the Peak Density Δ(A):**
      - **Heuristic Theorem 4.2:** The density of spike indices \( \leq A \) follows \( \Delta(A) \sim C' \cdot \frac{A}{\log A} \cdot f(\log\log A) \), with \( C' \) related to \( C \) and \( f \) a known arithmetic function. Argument via the Weil exponential sum heuristic and the Hardy-Littlewood conjectures. *This section bridges the algorithmic spike phenomenon to the arithmetic of prime tuples.*

**5. Discovery #7 & N10: Farey Universality (5-6 pages)**
   - **5.1 Context:** Analysis of the pseudospectrum near false singularities.
   - **5.2 Discovery #7: Universal Cluster Size 2:**
      - **Theorem 5.1:** For the eigenvalue problem embedded in MUSIC, near false zeros of the cost function, the non-trivial zeros cluster in pairs, independent of system parameters. Proof via saddle-point analysis on the characteristic polynomial.
   - **5.3 N10: Farey Gaps Outside Wigner-Dyson:**
      - **Observation 5.2:** The distribution of gap sizes between these clustered zero-pairs, when indexed by their position along the real line, does not follow random matrix theory (GUE/GOE). Instead, it is isomorphic to the distribution of gaps between Farey fractions of order \( N \) as \( N \to \infty \).
      - **Conjecture 5.3 (Farey Universality):** The statistical structure of spurious singularities in any high-dimensional sparse estimation problem based on a harmonic model (like MUSIC) is governed by the arithmetic of Farey sequences, not by eigenvalue repulsion of random matrices. *This is the grand unifying mathematical conjecture.*

**6. Synthesis and Implications (2-3 pages)**
   - How the arithmetic discoveries (C, Spike Rule, Farey gaps) directly explain the empirical performance of the MUSIC L-zero tomography algorithm (e.g., sources of failure, performance limits).
   - Discussion: The "why" behind the connection. Hypothesis: The MUSIC cost function's denominator is a product over prime-indexed spatial frequencies, injecting multiplicative number theory directly into the analytic landscape.

**7. Conclusion and Future Work (1 page)**
   - Summary of the dual contribution: (1) A new, performance-guaranteed sparse MUSIC estimator; (2) Discovery of a "Farey universality" in the errors of harmonic-based estimation.
   - Clear paths to formal separate papers:
      - *Paper A:* Full rigorous proof of the CRB and L-zero MUSIC algorithm.
      - *Paper B:* Complete analytic number theory treatment of the constant C and the spike rule, elevating Heuristic Theorem 4.2 to a theorem.
      - *Paper C:* Proof of the Farey gap universality (Theorem 5.1 is the key lemma) and exploration of its consequences in other inverse problems (e.g., compressed sensing).
