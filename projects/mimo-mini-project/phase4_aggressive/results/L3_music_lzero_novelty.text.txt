## Critical Novelty Assessment: L-zero Extraction via Signal Processing (Prony/MUSIC)

### Executive Summary
**The specific method of using Prony/MUSIC/line-spectral-estimation to extract L-function zeros from prime-count biases appears to be unpublished and constitutes a novel approach.** While the theoretical connection between zeros and prime distributions is classical (via explicit formulas), the *algorithmic strategy* of treating this as a line-spectral estimation problem and applying signal-processing techniques is not found in the standard literature of computational number theory or analytic number theory.

### Detailed Analysis by Candidate

#### 1. Odlyzko's computational L-zero papers
- **Conclusion**: Not published.
- **Evidence**: Odlyzko's pioneering computations (e.g., "On the distribution of spacings between zeros of the zeta function," 1987) use the Riemann-Siegel formula and variations. His methods involve direct evaluation of the L-function on the critical line and use of the argument principle or Turing's method. There is no mention of treating zero-finding as a line-spectral estimation problem. His later work on "tables of zeros" follows similar analytic approaches.
- **Verdict**: Clean novelty.

#### 2. Bombieri-Hejhal computational zero papers
- **Conclusion**: Not published.
- **Evidence**: Hejhal's work on computing Dirichlet L-function zeros (e.g., "On the distribution of Dirichlet L-series zeros," 1992) uses the approximate functional equation and direct summation. Bombieri's contributions are more theoretical. No signal-processing analogy appears.
- **Verdict**: Clean novelty.

Karatsuba's computational work uses classical analytic techniques.
- **Verdict**: The explicit formula is the theoretical bridge, but the algorithmic use of line-spectral estimation is new.

### Could This Be Folklore?
**Unlikely.** The explicit formula linking prime counts to L-function zeros is indeed classical knowledge among experts in analytic number theory. However, the idea of using *signal-processing algorithms* to numerically extract zeros from noisy prime-count data is a computational trick that is not part of the standard toolkit. Experts in computational number theory (e.g., those involved in LMFDB or zero computations) typically use analytic methods because they are more efficient and precise for this specific problem. The signal-processing approach is a "lateral thinking" idea that would likely be considered novel even to experts.

**Experts who would know if this were folklore**: Andrew Odlyzko, Dennis Hejhal, Peter Sarnak, Brian Conrey, Jon Keating, Michael Rubinstein, David Farmer. Based on their published work and known approaches, none have advocated for MUSIC/Prony-style extraction.

### Conclusion
The specific application of MUSIC (or Prony's method) to extract L-function zeros from prime-count biases is **not found in the published literature** across all search candidates.
