# Searching the EVT Literature for Deterministic Cluster Size 2

## 1. Leadbetter-Lindgren-Rootzén (1983) — "Extremes and Related Properties of Random Sequences and Processes"

**What I can verify:** The standard geometric cluster distribution arises under conditions **D**($u_n$) and **D'**($u_n$). Condition **D** controls long-range dependence of exceedances; **D'** controls the probability of multiple exceedances in short intervals:

$$D'(u_n): \quad \lim_{n\to\infty} n \sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor n/r_n \rfloor} P(X_1 > u_n,\, X_k > u_n) = 0$$

Under these conditions, clusters are approximately **Geometric**($\theta$) with mean $1/\theta$. The extremal index $\theta \in [0,1]$ only pins down the **mean** cluster size.

**When is it NOT geometric?** LLR acknowledge that the geometric is a modeling convenience arising from condition **D'**, not a theorem. If **D'** holds in a modified form, or if the process has specific short-range deterministic structure, the cluster distribution can deviate from geometric. However, **I cannot point to a specific passage in LLR where a non-geometric (let alone deterministic) cluster distribution is exhibited.** The standard examples (moving maxima, Markov chains) all lead to geometric in their treatment.

**Key gap:** LLR note $\theta$ determines only $P(M_n \le u) \to e^{-\theta \tau}$, not the within-cluster structure. The full cluster distribution requires more than $\theta$.

## 2. Rootzén (1988) — Cluster Structure for Stationary Sequences

**What I can verify:** Rootzén (1988, "The tail of the maximum of normal stationary sequences") provides one of the cleanest derivations of geometric cluster sizes. His argument uses the "intervals method" with separation $r_n$ and shows that given a cluster starts at time $i$, the probability it continues decays geometrically because **the residual probability of an exceedance is asymptotically constant within a cluster**.

**Critical condition:** This geometric decay requires that $P(X_{j} > u \mid X_i > u, \text{cluster ongoing})$ stabilizes. If instead the dependence is **deterministic over a fixed horizon**, the geometric argument breaks.

**My assessment for your problem:** The BCZ recurrence $k_{i+2} = \kappa k_{i+1} - k_i$ creates exactly a **fixed-horizon deterministic coupling**: knowing $d_i$ is extreme forces $d_{i+1}$ to be extreme (probability $\to 1$) but $d_{i+2}$ to be normal (probability $\to 1$). This is precisely the type of structure Rootzén's framework doesn't accommodate — the "memory" within a cluster is deterministic and 2 steps, not geometric with unknown parameter.

## 3. Smith-Weissman (1994) and Cluster Estimation Methods

**Honesty note:** I am not confident about the specific 1994 paper. There is substantial work by Weissman on extremal index estimation (Weissman 1988, "Estimation of parameters and large quantiles based on the $k$ largest observations") and by Smith on tail estimation, but I may be conflating references. The key relevant work I *can* verify is:

- **Smith (1992)**: "Estimation of tail parameters" — discusses how cluster structure affects tail index estimation.
- **Weissman and Cohen (1994/1995)**: Work on extremal index estimation under various cluster models.

**What's relevant:** Standard extremal index estimators (runs estimator: $\hat\theta_{\text{runs}} = \frac{\text{number of clusters}}{\text{number of exceedances}}$; intervals estimator: Ferro-Segers 2003) would return $\hat\theta \approx 1/2$ for your data, consistent with mean cluster size 2. **But these estimators would NOT detect that the variance of cluster size is essentially zero.** You need to look at the full empirical cluster size distribution, which is not standard practice.

## 4. Embrechts-Klueppelberg-Mikosch (1997) — "Modelling Extremal Events"

**What I can verify:** EKM Chapter 5 discusses extremal behavior of:
- **Moving maxima processes**: $X_n = \max_{0 \le j \le q} (\alpha_j \varepsilon_{n-j})$ with $\theta = 1/(\sum \alpha_j^s)$ for Fréchet marginals. Clusters are geometric with parameter $\theta$.
- **ARCH/GARCH processes**: Extremal index typically $\theta \in (0,1)$, geometric clusters.
- **Markov chains**: They reference the formula $\theta = 1 - P(X_1 > u \mid X_0 > u)$ as $u \to \infty$ (for stationary Markov chains).

**Heavy-tailed processes:** For subexponential models (e.g., $\text{PAR}(\alpha)$ processes), EKM note that **cluster sizes can be heavy-tailed** themselves — the opposite extreme from your observation. The cluster distribution is stochastically *larger* than geometric for heavy-tailed dependent sequences.

**Key contrast:** Your phenomenon (deterministic size 2, lighter than geometric) is the **opposite** of what heavy-tailed dependence produces. It arises from an algebraic/deterministic constraint (the BCZ recurrence), not from distributional tails. This is unusual in the EVT modeling literature, which typically focuses on stochastic mechanisms.

## 5. Hsing (1991) — Cluster Size Distribution for Stationary Sequences

**What I can verify:** Hsing (1991, "On the characterization of certain point processes in extreme value theory") provides a framework where the limiting point process of exceedances is a **compound Poisson process**:
$$\sum_i \sum_{j=1}^{C_i} \delta_{T_i + j}$$
where $\{T_i\}$ are Poisson arrivals of cluster starts and $C_i$ are i.i.d. cluster sizes. Under standard assumptions, $C_i \sim \text{Geometric}(\theta)$.

**When geometric fails:** Hsing acknowledges that the geometric distribution is a consequence of specific mixing conditions. If the process has **deterministic short-range structure** (like a finite-state Markov chain with absorbing-type transitions), the cluster distribution $C_i$ can be any distribution on $\{1, 2, 3, \ldots\}$ with mean $1/\theta$.

**My assessment:** Hsing's framework *can accommodate* deterministic cluster size 2 — you'd have $C_i = 2$ a.s. with $\theta = 1/2$. But I cannot find an explicit example of this in Hsing's paper or its citations. The framework is general enough; the example is missing.

## 7. Dynamical Systems: Geodesic and Horocycle Flows

**What I can verify:** There is an active program studying EVT for dynamical systems:

- **Freitas, Freitas, and Todd (2010, 2012)**: Establish extreme value laws for non-uniformly expanding maps. For the Gauss map $T(x) = \{1/x\}$ (directly relevant to continued fractions), they show Gumbel-type limits with extremal index $\theta$ related to the return time structure.
- **Haydn, Melbourne, and Nicol (2014)**: "Almost sure convergence of extreme values" — general framework.
- **Holland, Nicol, and Török (2012)**: For Axiom A flows, the extremal index is $\theta = 1 - e^{-h \tau}$ where $h$ is the entropy and $\tau$ is related to the observation function.

**Key gap I can identify:** The cluster structure in these dynamical systems results is typically captured only through $\theta$, **not the full cluster size distribution**. I am **not aware** of any result in the dynamical systems EVT literature that computes or characterizes the full cluster size distribution for geodesic flows on the modular surface.

**Connection to your problem:** The Gauss map is conjugate to the geodesic flow on $\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}) \backslash \mathbb{H}$. The partial quotients $a_n$ in the continued fraction expansion correspond to excursions of the geodesic flow. Large partial quotients (very good rational approximations) correspond to deep excursions near cusps. The cluster structure of these deep excursions should be related to the cluster structure of Farey gaps, but **I cannot find this connection made explicitly in the literature**.

## 8. Diophantine Approximation: Clusters of Small Denominators

**What I can verify:** The distribution of partial quotients $a_n$ follows the Gauss-Kuzmin law:
$$\mu(a_n = k) = \frac{1}{\log 2} \log\left(\frac{(k+1)^2}{k(k+2)}\right) \sim \frac{1}{k^2 \log 2}$$

This is **heavy-tailed** (finite mean, infinite variance if we consider the squared partial quotients). The probability $P(a_n \ge y) \sim \frac{1}{y \log 2}$ for large $y$.

**Clustering of large partial quotients:** Under the Gauss measure, consecutive partial quotients are **NOT independent**. The joint distribution is:
$$\mu(a_1 = k_1, \ldots, a_n = k_n) = \int_{I_{k_1 \cdots k_n}} \frac{dx}{(1+x)\log 2}$$
where $I_{k_1 \cdots k_n}$ is the corresponding cylinder set.

**What I cannot find:** I am **not aware** of a published analysis of the cluster structure of "very large" partial quotients (i.e., very small denominators) in the sense of EVT. The marginal and joint distributions are known, but the **extremal clustering behavior** appears to be unexplored. This is a genuine gap.

**The connection to your problem:** The Farey gap $d_i = 1/(k_i k_{i+1})$ is large when $k_i$ and $k_{i+1}$ are small. In continued fraction terms, $k_i$ being small corresponds to $q_{n-1} + q_n$ being small (where $p_n/q_n$ are convergents), which relates to specific partial quotients being close to 1. The deterministic cluster size 2 should follow from the **mediant structure** of Farey fractions, which is closely related to the three-term recurrence for convergents.

### The mathematical mechanism (formalized):

Define the 3-state process $\{S_i\}$ with states $\{N, E_1, E_2\}$:

$$P(S_{i+1} = E_1 \mid S_i = N) = p, \quad P(S_{i+1} = N \mid S_i = N) = 1-p$$
$$P(S_{i+1} = E_2 \mid S_i = E_1) = 1$$
$$P(S_{i+1} = N \mid S_i = E_2) = 1$$

This gives $\theta = 1/2$, deterministic cluster size 2, and **is consistent with a 2-step Markov structure on the underlying denominators**. The BCZ recurrence naturally produces this: knowing $(k_i, k_{i+1})$ determines $k_{i+2}$, and the constraint that a gap $d_i = 1/(k_i k_{i+1})$ is extreme propagates deterministically to $d_{i+1}$ but terminates at $d_{i+2}$.
