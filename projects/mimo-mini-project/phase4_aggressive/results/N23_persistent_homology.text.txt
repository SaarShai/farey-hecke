Persistent homology is unlikely to reveal a new invariant for the pairwise Farey gap point cloud \(P_N = \{(d_i, d_{i+1})\}\) because the limiting support—the triangle \(x + y > 1\)—is contractible. As \(N\) grows, \(P_N\) becomes a dense, approximately uniform sample from this triangle, whose Betti numbers are \(\beta_0 = 1\), \(\beta_1 = 0\), and \(\beta_{\geq 2} = 0\). Consequently, the persistence diagram for \(\beta_1\) will contain only short-lived features (noise) with no persistent loops, making it indistinguishable from that of any other dense sample from the same triangle. The BCZ recurrence and discrete rational structure of finite \(N\) may produce small-scale lattice effects, but these vanish in the limit and do not yield a robust, scale-invariant distinguishing feature.

To seek a potentially richer invariant, consider extending the analysis to 3-tuple distributions \(Q_N = \{(d_i, d_{i+1}, d_{i+2})\}\) in \([0,1]^3\). The joint support of three consecutive Farey gaps is a polytope defined by linear constraints (e.g., from the three-term recurrence), which may have nontrivial topology—such as holes or higher-dimensional cavities—that persistent homology could detect. For instance, the space might resemble a 2-complex with non-zero \(\beta_1\) or \(\beta_2\), reflecting hidden relationships not present in independent uniform samples. If persistent homology of \(Q_N\) yields long-lived features absent in random point clouds with the same marginal distributions, this would constitute a new topological invariant of the Farey sequence.

Look for persistent \(\beta_1\) or \(\beta_2\) features that appear robust across scales and absent in controls.
