We are estimating the frequencies γ_k (imaginary parts of zeros ρ_k = β_k + iγ_k) of an L-function from observations of ψ_L(x) at discrete x values. The explicit formula (under suitable hypotheses) gives:

ψ_L(x) − x ≈ -∑_{ρ} x^ρ / ρ

Take logs: let t = log x, so x = e^t. Then:

ψ_L(e^t) − e^t ≈ -∑_{ρ} e^{(β+iγ)t} / ρ

We are interested in estimating the γ_k. Assume RH: β_k = 1/2 for all nontrivial zeros. Then:

ψ_L(e^t) − e^t ≈ -∑_{γ} e^{(1/2 + iγ)t} / (1/2 + iγ)

We can absorb the factor 1/(1/2+iγ) into the amplitude. So we have a sum of complex exponentials with frequencies γ_k and complex amplitudes A_k = -1/(1/2+iγ_k) = -1/|ρ_k| e^{-i arg(ρ_k)}.

So, to summarize:

1. The CR bound for estimating the k-th zero γ_k, when modeling the signal as a sum of sinusoids up to frequency Γ, with higher frequencies treated as white noise of variance σ^2 ≈ (2/π)(log(Γ/2π)+1)/Γ, is:

var(γ̂_k) ≥ \frac{12 σ^2}{a_k^2 T^3} = \frac{12 σ^2 |ρ_k|^2}{T^3} ≈ \frac{12 σ^2 γ_k^2}{T^3}.

2. MUSIC is a subspace method that achieves the CR bound for frequency estimation in white noise when the signal-to-noise ratio is high and the frequencies are well-separated. So we expect MUSIC to be near-optimal.

3. I am not aware of this specific CR bound being published. It combines classical signal processing CR bound with number-theoretic explicit formula. So likely novel.

4. To predict the minimum X for 1% precision on the k-th zero, we need to specify N and X_min. Assume N=100, X_min=100, and we include up to Γ such that the number of zeros up to Γ is about N/2=50.
