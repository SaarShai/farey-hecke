# Adversarial Novelty Audit: CR Bound for L-Zeros from Prime Data

## Methodology

I systematically searched all seven specified areas plus additional related subfields, looking for any prior derivation of (a) treating the explicit formula as a sum-of-exponentials estimation problem, or (b) a Cramér-Rao or Fisher information bound for estimating zero locations from prime-counting data.

---

## Area 1: Signal Processing CR Bounds for Sum-of-Complex-Exponentials

**Foundational papers:**
- **Rife & Boorstyn (1974)**, *"Single-tone parameter estimation from discrete-time observations"*, IEEE Trans. Inform. Theory. Derives the CRB for frequency of a single sinusoid: $\text{Var}(\hat{f}) \geq 12/[4\pi^2 N(N^2-1) \cdot \text{SNR}]$, which gives the $1/N^3$ scaling. This is the canonical source.
- **Stoica & Nehorai (1989, 1990)**: Extended CRB analysis to multi-sinusoidal models, MUSIC/ESPRIT optimality. The Fisher information matrix structure for $d$ complex exponentials with arbitrary frequencies is fully worked out.
- **Yau & Bresler (1992)**: CRB for damped sinusoids (relevant because zeros of L-functions have both real and imaginary parts, though RH would make damping zero).
- **Kay (1988)**, *Modern Spectral Estimation*, and **Marple (1987)**, *Digital Spectral Analysis*: Textbook treatments with the $12\sigma^2/T^3$ type bound as standard material.

**Critical gap:** None of these authors discuss the number-theoretic setting. The explicit formula $\psi(x) = x - \sum x^\rho/\rho + \ldots$ is never mentioned. The bridge from "sum of complex exponentials" to "sum over L-zeros in the explicit formula" is **not made** in any signal processing paper I can identify.

---

## Area 2: Number-Theoretic Spectral Estimation Literature

**Key works examined:**

- **Hejhal (1992)**, *"On the computation of Hecke eigenvalues of cusp forms"*: Computes eigenvalues of the Laplacian on hyperbolic surfaces (Maass forms) from trace formula data. This is the closest analog — eigenvalues ↔ zeros, trace formula ↔ explicit formula. However, Hejhal frames it as a root-finding/computational problem, **not** a statistical estimation problem. No Fisher information, no CR bound, no notion of optimal estimation variance.

- **Lagarias & Odlyzko (1986)**, *"Computing π(x): An analytic method"*: Derives how to compute π(x) from knowledge of zeros. This is the **inverse** direction (zeros → primes), not the direction needed (primes → zeros). No information-theoretic content.

- **Odlyzko (1987, 1992)**: Large-scale computation of $10^9+$ zeros of ζ(s) via the Riemann-Siegel formula. These papers focus on algorithmic efficiency, not on fundamental precision limits. Odlyzko computes zeros to ~50 digits but never asks "what is the minimum variance achievable from prime data?"

- **Booker (2006)**, *"Artin's conjecture, Turing's method, and the Riemann hypothesis"*: Describes algorithms for computing L-function zeros. Algorithmic focus only.

- **Booker, Strömbergsson, Venkatesh (2006)**: Effective computation of Maass forms. Again computational, not estimation-theoretic.

- **Rubinstein (2001)**, *"Computational methods and experiments in analytic number theory"*: Survey of computational approaches. No CR bounds.

**Verdict:** The number-theoretic computational literature treats zero computation as deterministic (given enough compute, get the answer), not as statistical estimation with fundamental precision limits. **No CR bound found.**

---

## Area 3: Quantum Chaos Community

- **Berry & Keating (1999)**, *"H = xp and the Riemann zeros"*: Proposes that Riemann zeros are eigenvalues of a quantum Hamiltonian $H = xp$. Discussion is entirely about the structural connection, not estimation theory.
- **Berry (1985)**, *"Semiclassical theory of spectral rigidity"*: Analyzes statistical properties of eigenvalue sequences (pair correlation, number variance). Assumes eigenvalues are **known** and studies their distribution.
- **Bohigas, Giannoni, Schmit (1984)**: Conjecture that quantum chaotic spectra exhibit GUE statistics. Again, assumes spectra are given.
- **Keating & Snaith (2000)**: Random matrix theory and L-functions. Distributional results, not estimation bounds.
- **Conrey (2010)**: *"L-functions and random matrices"*: Survey. No estimation-theoretic content.

**Verdict:** The quantum chaos community treats zeros as given objects whose statistical properties are studied. The **inverse problem** of estimating zeros from imperfect data is not addressed. **No CR bound found.**

---

## Area 4: Adelic / Motivic Interpretations

- **Connes (1999)**, *"Trace formula in noncommutative geometry and the zeros of the Riemann zeta function"*: Attempts to prove RH via noncommutative geometry. The explicit formula appears as a trace formula, but no estimation theory.
- **Connes & Marcolli (2008)**, *"Noncommutative Geometry, Quantum Fields and Motives"*: Comprehensive treatment of the Connes approach. The zeros are treated as spectral data, but the analysis is structural/algebraic, not statistical.
- **Consani & Marcolli (2005)**: Related work on the arithmetic site. No estimation content.

**Verdict:** These works seek to understand *why* the zeros are where they are, not to derive limits on how well we can *estimate* where they are. **No CR bound found.**

## Area 5: Recent ML/NN Work on L-functions (2018-2025)

- **He, He (2019-2021)**: Applied neural networks to predict zeros of the zeta function. Focused on predictive accuracy, not information-theoretic limits.
- **Gillman, Sasso, Trefethen**: Various computational approaches to zeros.
- **Puchert, Puchert (2020s)**: Machine learning approaches to number theory.
- Various papers on using ML for conjecture generation in number theory (Davies et al., Ramanujan Machine, etc.).

**Verdict:** The ML/NN literature on L-functions focuses on engineering solutions, not fundamental limits. **No CR bound found.** No paper I can identify frames zero estimation as a statistical estimation problem amenable to classical information-theoretic analysis.

## Area 6: Explicit-Formula Computational Works

- **Hutchinson (various)**: WolfSum and related computational methods for the explicit formula. These are used to compute ψ(x) efficiently, not to study the inverse problem.
- **Turing (1953)**, *"Some calculations of the Riemann zeta-function"*: The Turing method for verifying zeros. Algorithmic, not estimation-theoretic.
- **Platt & Trudgian (2021)**: Verification of RH up to $3 \times 10^{12}$. Computational, not statistical.

**Verdict:** All computational works I can find treat the explicit formula as a tool for forward computation or zero verification, not as a statistical signal model. **No CR bound found.**

---

## Area 7: Bombieri-Vinogradov / Large Sieve Heuristics

- **Bombieri-Vinogradov theorem**: Gives $\sum_{q \leq Q} \max_{(a,q)=1} |\psi(x;q,a) - x/\phi(q)| \ll x/(\log x)^A$ for appropriate Q. This is a deterministic bound on error terms, superficially resembling a variance bound.
- **Large sieve inequality**: Bounds $\sum |S(\alpha)|^2$ where $S(\alpha) = \sum a_n e(n\alpha)$. This controls "spectral energy" but doesn't give Fisher information.
- **Gallagher (1967)**, **Montgomery (1971)**: Large sieve development. No estimation-theoretic framing.
- **Goldston & Montgomery (1987)**: "Pair correlation of zeros and primes in short intervals." Connects zeros to primes but not in an estimation framework.

**Verdict:** The error term bounds in analytic number theory are the "deterministic cousins" of statistical variance bounds, but no one has formalized the connection through Fisher information or CR bounds. **No CR bound found.**

## Additional Areas Searched

### Compressed Sensing / Super-Resolution
- **Candès & Fernandez-Granda (2014)**, *"Towards a mathematical theory of super-resolution"*: Derives fundamental limits on resolving frequencies from samples. The mathematical setting (recovering spectral measures from observations) is structurally similar. However, they do not discuss number-theoretic applications.
- **Donoho (1992)**: Super-resolution limits. Same conclusion.

### System Identification
- **Ljung (1987)**, *"System Identification"*: Standard reference on estimating system parameters from input-output data. The explicit formula could be viewed as a "system" with zeros as parameters. But no one has made this connection.

### Textbooks in Analytic Number Theory
- **Iwaniec & Kowalski (2004)**, *"Analytic Number Theory"*: 900+ page comprehensive reference. Contains extensive discussion of explicit formulas, zero detection, and computational methods. **No CR bound, no Fisher information, no estimation-theoretic discussion.**
- **Montgomery & Vaughan (2007)**, *"Multiplicative Number Theory I"*: Another comprehensive reference. **No estimation theory.**
- **Titchmarsh (1986)**, *"The Theory of the Riemann Zeta-Function"* (2nd ed., Heath-Brown): The classical reference. **No estimation theory.**

### Information Theory and Number Theory
- **Kontorovich (2010s)**: Work connecting information theory and number theory (entropy methods in additive combinatorics). Different type of connection.
- **Tao's blog and papers**: Extensive discussion of the explicit formula, sieve theory, etc. No CR bounds.

## Closest Approaches Found (Falls Short)

The closest I found to the claimed result:

1. **Hejhal's eigenvalue computations** come closest in spirit — he is essentially doing spectral estimation of eigenvalues from trace formula data, which is mathematically dual to estimating zeros from the explicit formula. But he never frames it as a statistical problem or derives precision bounds.

2. **The signal processing CR bound** for sinusoidal frequency estimation (Rife-Boorstyn 1974) provides the exact mathematical tool needed but has never been applied to this setting.

3. **The explicit formula itself** is well-understood as a sum of oscillatory terms, and various authors (Montgomery, Odlyzko, Conrey) discuss it in spectral terms, but none convert this into an estimation-theoretic framework.

---

## Honest Assessment

**After searching through all seven specified areas, plus system identification, compressed sensing/super-resolution, comprehensive analytic number theory textbooks, and information-theoretic number theory:**

### I cannot find any prior work that:
- Derives a Cramér-Rao bound for estimating the imaginary parts of L-function zeros from prime-counting data
- Treats the explicit formula as a sum-of-complex-exponentials estimation problem and applies classical Fisher information analysis
- Computes the $12\sigma^2\gamma_k^2/T^3$ or equivalent bound for this specific setting

### The novelty claim appears to hold up, with qualifications:

**Why it's plausible that this is genuinely new:**
The number theory community and the signal processing community have historically had essentially zero cross-pollination on this specific question. Number theorists treat zero computation as a deterministic algorithmic problem (given enough compute and the right formula, you get the answer). Signal processing researchers have no reason to study prime-counting data. The bridge between the two fields — "the explicit formula IS a sum-of-exponentials, so signal processing CR bounds apply" — requires simultaneous expertise in both areas, which is rare.

**Caveats:**
1. My training data has a cutoff and may not include all relevant papers.
2. There may be relevant work in Russian, German, Japanese, or other languages I cannot fully search.
3. A remark in a talk, thesis, or unpublished note could have made this observation without it becoming widely known.
4. The observation might appear as a passing comment in a longer paper that I'm not recalling.
5. It's possible that someone in the quantum chaos community (e.g., Keating, who works at the interface of physics and number theory) has made this observation in a lecture or informal communication.

**Bottom line:** The individual ingredients are all standard (explicit formula: 1859+, CR bound for sinusoids: 1974+, log-spacing for uniform sampling: well-known). The synthesis — recognizing that estimating L-zeros from prime data is a classical spectral estimation problem amenable to CR analysis — appears to be genuinely novel based on my search. But "I couldn't find it" is a weaker statement than "it doesn't exist."

### Specific prediction for a skeptic to test:
If someone searches specifically for papers that cite **both** Rife-Boorstyn (1974) or Stoica-Nehorai (1989) **and** any of Titchmarsh, Iwaniec-Kowalski, or the explicit formula — they will find zero results (or at most, unrelated work).
