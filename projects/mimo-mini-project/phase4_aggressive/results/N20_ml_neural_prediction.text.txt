**Assessment.**  
The proposal is **novel and worth exploring**, though not without precedent in a broader sense. While ML has been applied to aspects of the Riemann zeta function (e.g., approximating zeros via neural networks trained on partial sums of the Dirichlet series, or using symbolic regression to discover connections), the specific idea of learning the mapping from *prime-bias signals* to L-function zeros across a family of L-functions using modern sequence models appears underexplored. The closest related work may be in using ML for solving inverse problems in analytic number theory or physics, but framing it explicitly as a sequence-to-sequence learning task (prime-bias input → zeros output) is a fresh angle. It aligns with a growing trend of “mathematics through ML” (as in DeepMind’s work), but with a focus on a core object in analytic number theory.

**Pursuing this is worthwhile** because it tests whether neural architectures can capture structure beyond the harmonic-retrieval framework of MUSIC. MUSIC is optimal for superimposed sinusoids, but the prime-bias signal of an L-function is not a pure sum of exponentials (it’s a sum over primes with Dirichlet coefficients and zeros acting as frequencies). A neural model could potentially learn priors about the distribution of zeros across families of L-functions, or discover correlations between prime statistics and zero locations that are not explicitly encoded in Prony-like methods. A key question is whether ML can generalize to L-functions outside the training distribution (e.g., those with unusual conductor or sign) or handle limited, noisy prime data—MUSIC requires perfect knowledge of the signal on a grid, but real applications may have gaps or noise.

**Fastest path to a publishable result:**  
1. **Dataset**: Use LMFDB’s database of L-functions (focus on ones with completed zeros and prime-counting data available via its API). Augment with synthetically generated L-functions from known families (e.g., twists, symmetric powers) to reach ~2000+ examples. Represent prime-bias as a vector of values at log-spaced points (as specified).  
2. **Baselines**:  
   - MUSIC as the primary analytic baseline.  
   - Simpler ML models: 1D CNNs, LSTMs to establish if neural nets can even match MUSIC.  
   - Transformer on the sequence of prime-bias values (with positional encoding for log-spacing).  
3. **Evaluation**:  
   - Primary metric: Relative error in predicted zeros (especially low-lying ones).  
   - Generalization test: Train on L-functions with conductor ≤ N, test on those with conductor > N.  
   - Noise robustness: Add synthetic noise to prime-bias signals.  
4. **Publishable angle**: If the neural model outperforms MUSIC on out-of-family or noisy data, or if it can predict zeros with only partial prime-bias data (e.g., only up to 10⁴), that’s a strong result. Even a negative result (ML doesn’t beat MUSIC) could be interesting if it explains *why* (e.g., due to the arithmetic nature of the signals).  

**Caveat**: The main risk is that the problem is too “clean” for ML—MUSIC might already be near-optimal under perfect, full data. The exciting case is if ML shows robustness in realistic, imperfect scenarios.
