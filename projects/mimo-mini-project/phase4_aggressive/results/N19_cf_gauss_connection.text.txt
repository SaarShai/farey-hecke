#### 1. **Lag-1 Correlation = 1/2 for CF Partial Quotients \( (a_n) \)?**
   - **Reasoning**: In the Farey sequence, your finding of lag-1 correlation = +1/2 for gaps (likely referring to normalized differences \( g_n = x_{n+1} - x_n \) in the Farey sequence of order \( N \), as \( N \to \infty \)) suggests a level of attraction. For CF, the partial quotients \( a_n \) are determined by the Gauss map \( a_n = \lfloor 1/x_{n-1} \rfloor \), with \( x_n = \{1/x_{n-1}\} \). Under the Gauss measure \( d\mu = \frac{1}{\ln 2} \frac{dx}{1+x} \) on [0,1], the \( a_n \) are identically distributed but **not independent**. However, the marginal distribution \( P(a_n = k) = \frac{1}{\ln 2} \log\left(1 + \frac{1}{k(k+2)}\right) \) has a heavy tail \( \sim \frac{1}{(\ln 2) k^2} \) for large \( k \), leading to **infinite mean and variance** (since \( E[a_n] = \infty \)). Consequently, the standard Pearson lag-1 correlation \( \text{Cor}(a_n, a_{n+1}) = \frac{\text{Cov}(a_n, a_{n+1})}{\sqrt{\text{Var}(a_n) \text{Var}(a_{n+1})}} \) is **undefined** due to divergent moments.
   
   - **Testable prediction**: To define correlation meaningfully, consider normalized or transformed variables (e.g., \( \log a_n \) or \( a_n^{-1} \)). For \( a_n^{-1} \), \( E[a_n^{-1}] \) converges, and joint distributions can be computed. Using the Gauss-Kuzmin distribution, the joint probability \( P(a_1 = k, a_2 = m) \approx \frac{1}{\ln 2} \int_{a}^{b} \frac{dx}{1+x} \), where \( a = \frac{1}{k + 1/m} \), \( b = \frac{1}{k + 1/(m+1)} \). For large \( k, m \), \( P(k, m) \sim \frac{1}{(\ln 2) k^2 m^2} \), while \( P(a_1 = k) P(a_2 = m) \sim \frac{1}{(\ln 2)^2 k^2 m^2} \), implying asymptotic dependence but no fixed correlation like 1/2. Empirically, simulate sequences under Gauss measure to compute lag-1 autocorrelation of \( \log a_n \) or other transforms; if it converges to a constant, it may resemble 1/2, but no known result confirms this.
   
   - **Conclusion for CF**: No known lag-1 correlation = 1/2 for \( a_n \). The heavy-tailed, infinite-variance nature prevents direct comparison to Farey gaps. If your Farey lag-1 = 1/2 is robust, the CF analog may require a different observable (e.g., gap-related via \( q_n \)), as Farey gaps \( \sim \frac{1}{q_n q_{n+1}} \) where \( q_n \) grows like \( e^{\gamma n} \) (Lévy's constant \( \gamma = \frac{\pi^2}{12 \ln 2} \)).

#### 2. **Cluster-Size = 2 for Extreme Partial Quotients?**
   - **Reasoning**: In Farey sequences, extreme gaps (very small or large) exhibit clustering with size 2, meaning pairs of consecutive extremes are common. For CF, "extreme partial quotients" likely refer to large \( a_n \) (record values or high thresholds \( u \)). The tail probability \( P(a_n > u) \sim \frac{1}{(\ln 2) u} \) suggests Pareto-like behavior (tail index 1). However, the Gauss map is **uniformly expanding and mixing** with exponential decay of correlations for Hölder observables, implying little clustering for extremes.
   
   - **Testable prediction**: Compute the extremal index \( \theta \) for exceedances over \( u \). For i.i.d. Pareto with tail \( 1/u \), cluster size is geometric with mean \( 1/\theta \), and \( \theta = 1 \) means no clustering (size 1). Under Gauss measure, \( P(a_{n+1} > u \mid a_n > u) \approx \frac{P(a_1 > u, a_2 > u)}{P(a_1 > u)} \). From asymptotic joint \( P(a_1 > u, a_2 > u) \sim \frac{1}{(\ln 2) u^2} \) and marginal \( P(a_1 > u) \sim \frac{1}{(\ln 2) u} \), we get \( P(a_{n+1} > u \mid a_n > u) \sim \frac{1}{u} \approx P(a_{n+1} > u) \), indicating \( \theta \approx 1 \). Thus, **no clustering** (cluster-size = 1). This holds even for small \( a_n \) (e.g., \( a_n = 1 \)), where conditional probabilities show slight negative correlation (e.g., \( P(a_{n+1}=1 \mid a_n=1) \approx 0.366 < P(a_n=1) \approx 0.415 \)).
   
   - **Conclusion for CF**: Cluster-size for extreme \( a_n \) is likely 1, not 2. If your Farey result holds, the correspondence may not preserve this structure due to the fast mixing of the Gauss map. Alternatively, "extremes" in CF might refer to minimal \( a_n \) (e.g., \( a_n = 1 \)) or sequences of denominators \( q_n \), but simulation is needed to check for size-2 clusters.
