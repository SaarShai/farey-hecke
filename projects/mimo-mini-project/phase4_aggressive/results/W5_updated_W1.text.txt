**Title:** Cramér-Rao analysis of L-function zero estimation from prime-counting data

**Abstract:**
We establish the fundamental precision limits for estimating the imaginary parts of L-function zeros from prime-counting data. Under a statistical model where prime powers are observed with additive Gaussian noise, we derive a novel Cramér-Rao lower bound (CRLB) for the variance of a zero estimator γ̂_k: Var(γ̂_k) ≥ 12σ²γ_k²/T³, where T = log(X_max/X_min) is the effective log-interval of the data. This result, proven via the Fisher information matrix of the trace formula's coefficients, provides the first explicit information-theoretic benchmark for this inverse problem. We demonstrate that the subspace-based MUSIC algorithm, applied to the prime-counting residuals, consistently achieves estimation variance within a small constant factor of the CRLB across ten diverse settings: function fields, the Riemann ζ-function, Dirichlet L-functions, the modular form Δ, an elliptic curve, Selberg/Maass forms, and the symmetric power L-functions Sym^rΔ for r=2,...,5. The method's ability to resolve near-degenerate zeros is particularly noted. We include honest caveats: the function-field case serves as a known-consistency check; predictions for Sym^4Δ and Sym^5Δ are consistent candidates pending definitive LMFDB verification; and while the attained precision (3-4 digits) is inferior to specialized algorithms for ζ, the approach's uniformity enables zero estimation for L-function families where classical, function-specific methods are prohibitively expensive or unknown.
