## Prony's Method and Spectral Estimation

This is precisely the classical problem of **harmonic retrieval** or **frequency estimation** in signal processing. Given:
$$s_n = \sum_{j=1}^d c_j z_j^n, \quad n = 0, 1, \ldots, N-1$$

where $z_j = e^{i\theta_j}$ (or more generally, $z_j$ are distinct complex numbers), recover the $z_j$ (and possibly the $c_j$).

**Prony's method** (1895): From $2d$ samples, one can exactly recover $d$ complex exponentials. The method works by finding the annihilating filter: if $p(z) = \prod_{j=1}^d (z - z_j) = z^d + a_{d-1}z^{d-1} + \cdots + a_0$, then:
$$\sum_{k=0}^d a_k s_{n+k} = 0 \quad \text{for all } n$$

This gives a system of $d$ linear equations in $d$ unknowns ($a_0, \ldots, a_{d-1}$), solvable from $s_0, s_1, \ldots, s_{2d-1}$.

So $N = 2d$ is both necessary and sufficient (in the noiseless case) for exact recovery.

In the noisy case, MUSIC (MUltiple SIgnal Classification, Schmidt 1986) and ESPRIT (Roy-Kailath 1989) are more robust spectral estimation methods.
