We are given a narrative about the Farey sequence of order N, denoted F_N, which consists of all reduced fractions a/b with 0 ≤ a ≤ b ≤ N, sorted in increasing order. The founding observation is that when we go from F_{p-1} to F_p for a prime p, the new fractions k/p (k=1,...,p-1) are perfectly equispaced with gap 1/p. For composite N, the new fractions are not equispaced.

Additionally, there is an observation about the top quantile gaps (gaps at quantile q ≥ 0.9999) in F_N clustering in maximal runs of exactly 2. That is, extreme gaps (the largest ones) tend to occur in pairs: two large adjacent gaps separated by a fraction with relatively small denominator.

The hypothesis is that these two phenomena are connected: the extreme gaps arise from fractions with small denominators, and for a prime p, each such fraction a/p (with a coprime to p) creates two adjacent large gaps. So the cluster size of 2 is due to the fact that each such fraction has two neighbors, and both gaps tend to be large when the fraction has a small denominator.

We are asked to:
1. Make the connection precise.
2. Show that this is the original "prime-equispaced" observation in extreme-value form.
3. Identify what proof technique would make this rigorous.
4. Predict what happens as q → 1 (extreme limit).

Let's break it down.

**Step 2: Connection to primes.**

When we add a prime p to the Farey sequence, we insert all fractions k/p for k=1,...,p-1 (in reduced form, but since p is prime, they are all reduced except when k is multiple of p, but k<p so all are reduced). These fractions are equispaced with gap 1/p. But importantly, when p is large, these gaps 1/p are not extremely large; they are of order 1/p. For p to contribute to extreme gaps, we need 1/p to be comparable to 1/N, i.e., p to be small. So only small primes contribute to the extreme gaps. And for each such prime p, the fractions a/p (with a=1,...,p-1) are all in F_N for N ≥ p. Each such fraction has two adjacent gaps that are roughly 1/(p*N) (since the neighbors have denominators up to N). So each a/p generates a pair of extreme gaps if 1/(p*N) is above the threshold. So the number of such fractions is φ(p) = p-1. So the total number of extreme-gap pairs from prime p is p-1. And for composite denominators, similarly, fractions a/c with c small and gcd(a,c)=1 contribute. So the total number of extreme-gap pairs is roughly the sum over small b of φ(b). And since primes dominate for small b, most of these pairs come from prime denominators.

Thus, the original observation that primes insert equispaced fractions is now seen as the reason why extreme gaps come in pairs: each fraction from a small prime creates two large gaps.
