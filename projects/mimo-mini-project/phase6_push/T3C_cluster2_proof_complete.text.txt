## Theorem
Let \(F_N\) denote the Farey sequence of order \(N\). Fix a sequence of quantiles \(q_N\) such that \(1 - q_N = \kappa / N\) for some constant \(\kappa > 0\). As \(N \to \infty\), the probability that an extreme gap (i.e., a gap in the top \(q_N\) quantile) belongs to a cluster of exactly two consecutive extreme gaps tends to \(1\). Moreover, the probability of a cluster of size three or more tends to \(0\).

## Proof

### 1. Setup and Scaling
We rely on the Boca–Cobeli–Zaharescu (BCZ) equidistribution theorem: as \(N \to \infty\), the pairs of consecutive denominators \((b, d)\) in \(F_N\), normalized as \((x, y) = (b/N, d/N)\), become uniformly distributed on the triangle  
\[
T = \{(x, y) : 0 < x, y < 1,\ x + y > 1\}
\]  
with constant density \(2\).

The gap size between fractions with denominators \(b\) and \(d\) is \(1/(bd) = 1/(N^2 xy)\). Let \(\tau_N\) be the \((1 - q_N)\)-quantile of the gap distribution. An extreme gap satisfies  
\[
\frac{1}{N^2 xy} > \tau_N \quad \iff \quad xy < \frac{1}{N^2 \tau_N}.
\]  
Set \(\delta_N = 1/(N^2 \tau_N)\). Since \(1 - q_N = \kappa / N\) and the probability that a random \((x, y) \in T\) satisfies \(xy < \delta\) is asymptotically \(2\delta\) for small \(\delta\), we have \(2\delta_N \sim \kappa / N\), so \(\delta_N \sim \kappa/(2N)\). Thus \(\delta_N = O(1/N)\).

### 2. Structure of Extreme Gaps
For \((x, y) \in T\) with \(xy < \delta_N\), the condition \(x + y > 1\) forces one of the coordinates to be \(O(1/N)\). Indeed, if both \(x, y \geq \sqrt{\delta_N}\), then \(xy \geq \delta_N\), a contradiction. Hence at least one of \(x, y\) is \(<\sqrt{\delta_N} = O(1/\sqrt{N})\). Moreover, if \(x\) is not \(O(1/N)\), say \(x \geq A/N\) for some large \(A\), then \(y < \delta_N/x \leq \delta_N N / A = O(1/A)\). But then \(x + y \leq O(1/\sqrt{N}) + O(1/A) < 1\) for large \(N\) and \(A\), contradicting \(x + y > 1\). Therefore, for sufficiently large \(N\), every extreme gap satisfies either:

- **Type A:** \(x = O(1/N)\) and \(y = 1 - O(1/N)\) (i.e., \(y\) close to \(1\)),
- **Type B:** \(y = O(1/N)\) and \(x = 1 - O(1/N)\) (i.e., \(x\) close to \(1\)).

These two types are disjoint for large \(N\).

### 3. Cluster of Size 2
We analyze the neighbors of an extreme gap using the Stern–Brocot recurrences. Given a pair \((x, y)\), the next pair \((y, z)\) is determined by \(z = k y - x\) with \(k = \lfloor (1 + x)/y \rfloor\). The previous pair \((x', x)\) satisfies \(x' = k' x - y\) with \(k' = \lfloor (1 + y)/x \rfloor\).

**Claim 1 (Type B).** If \((x, y)\) is of Type B, then the next gap (corresponding to \((y, z)\)) is also extreme, but the previous gap (corresponding to \((x', x)\)) is not.

*Proof.* Write \(y = \beta/N\) with \(\beta = O(1)\) and \(x = 1 - \alpha/N\) with \(\alpha = O(1)\). Then  
\[
k = \left\lfloor \frac{1 + x}{y} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \frac{2 - \alpha/N}{\beta/N} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \frac{2N - \alpha}{\beta} \right\rfloor,
\]  
so \(k \sim 2N/\beta\). Hence  
\[
z = k y - x = \left( \frac{2N - \alpha}{\beta} + O(1) \right) \frac{\beta}{N} - (1 - \alpha/N) = 2 - \alpha/N + O(1/N) - 1 + \alpha/N = 1 + O(1/N).
\]  
Thus \(z \approx 1\). Consequently, \(y z \leq y = O(1/N)\), and since \(\delta_N = O(1/N)\), we have \(y z < \delta_N\) for large \(N\). Hence the next gap is extreme.

For the previous gap: \(k' = \lfloor (1 + y)/x \rfloor\). Since \(y = O(1/N)\) and \(x \approx 1\), \((1 + y)/x \approx 1 + y\), so \(k' = 1\) for large \(N\). Then \(x' = x - y = 1 - (\alpha + \beta)/N\). Thus  
\[
x' x = \left(1 - \frac{\alpha + \beta}{N}\right) \left(1 - \frac{\alpha}{N}\right) = 1 - O(1/N) = \Omega(1).
\]  
So \(x' x > \delta_N\) for large \(N\), and the previous gap is not extreme. \(\square\)

**Claim 2 (Type A).** If \((x, y)\) is of Type A, then the previous gap is extreme, but the next gap is not.

*Proof.* Symmetric to Claim 1. \(\square\)

Claims 1 and 2 show that every extreme gap is adjacent to exactly one other extreme gap, forming a pair. Thus the cluster size is at least 2.

### 4. No Clusters of Size 3
**Claim 3.** No three consecutive gaps can be extreme.

*Proof.* Suppose gaps corresponding to \((x, y)\), \((y, z)\), and \((z, w)\) are all extreme: \(xy < \delta_N\), \(yz < \delta_N\), \(zw < \delta_N\), with all sums \(> 1\).

- **Case 1:** \(y\) is small, i.e., \(y = O(1/N)\). Then as in Claim 1, \(x \approx 1\) and \(z \approx 1\). From \(zw < \delta_N\) and \(z \approx 1\), we get \(w = O(1/N)\). But the recurrence \(w = k' z - y\) with \(k' = \lfloor (1 + y)/z \rfloor \approx 1\) gives \(w = z - y \approx 1\), contradicting \(w = O(1/N)\).

- **Case 2:** \(y\) is not small. Then from \(xy < \delta_N\) we deduce \(x = O(1/N)\), and from \(yz < \delta_N\) we deduce \(z = O(1/N)\). Since \(x + y > 1\) and \(x\) is small, \(y \approx 1\); similarly, \(z + w > 1\) and \(z\) small implies \(w \approx 1\). Now consider the recurrence \(z = k y - x\). Since \(y \approx 1\) and \(x\) small, \(k = \lfloor (1 + x)/y \rfloor \approx 1\), so \(z = y - x \approx 1\), contradicting \(z = O(1/N)\).

Both cases lead to a contradiction, so three consecutive extreme gaps cannot occur. \(\square\)

### 5. Conclusion
Claims 1–3 show that with probability tending to \(1\) as \(N \to \infty\), an extreme gap is of Type A or Type B, and hence belongs to a cluster of exactly two consecutive extreme gaps. Clusters of size three or more have vanishing probability.

The proof relies on the BCZ equidistribution theorem (a rigorous result) and the asymptotic analysis of the recurrences under the condition \(\delta_N = O(1/N)\). No unproven hypotheses are needed beyond BCZ. \(\blacksquare\)
