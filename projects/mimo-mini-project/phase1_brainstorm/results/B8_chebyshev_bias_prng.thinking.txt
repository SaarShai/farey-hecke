## Understanding the Mathematical Background

**Chebyshev bias in function fields:**

In F_q[T], the analog of Chebyshev's bias is about the distribution of primes in arithmetic progressions. For a modulus M ∈ F_q[T] and residue classes a mod M with gcd(a, M) = 1, the prime-counting function π_{M,a}(X) (counting monic irreducible polynomials of degree ≤ n with degree-n part ≡ a mod M) deviates from uniform 1/φ(M) by an explicit, computable amount related to characters mod M and zeros of L-functions.

The key fact: the bias is *deterministic*, *computable*, and *small but exact*. In F_q[T], unlike Q, we have the Riemann hypothesis (proved by Weil/Deligne), so all nontrivial zeros of L-functions lie on the "critical line," and the bias has precise bounds.

For a monic irreducible polynomial f of degree n in F_q[T], the condition f ≡ a (mod M) can be detected. The count:

π_{M,a}(n) = (1/φ(M)) * q^n/n + (1/φ(M)) * Σ_χ χ(a^{-1}) * (sum over nontrivial zeros of L(s,χ))

where the sum over zeros gives an explicit bias term bounded by q^{n/2}/n.

**The bias structure:**
- Some residue classes are systematically over-represented, others under-represented.
- The sign and magnitude of the bias depend on a, M, and is computable via character sums/L-function values.
- This is NOT pseudorandom - it's deterministic number-theoretic structure.
