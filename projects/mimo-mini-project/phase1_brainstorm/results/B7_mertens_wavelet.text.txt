### 1. Concrete Definition of a "Mertens Transform"

The Mertens function \( M(n) = \sum_{k=1}^n \mu(k) \) is irregular and zero-mean. A natural wavelet-like basis on \( \mathbb{Z}^+ \) can be defined by **dilation and translation** of a "Mertens wavelet." We propose:

- **Mertens wavelet at scale \( N \):**  
  \[
  \psi_N(n) = M(n+N) - M(n) = \sum_{k=n+1}^{n+N} \mu(k).
  \]
  For \( N=1 \), \( \psi_1(n) = \mu(n+1) \). For larger \( N \), it is a moving sum of Möbius values.

- **Multiresolution basis:** Choose scales \( N_j = 2^j \) for \( j = 0, 1, \dots, J \), and define translated wavelets:
  \[
  \psi_{j,k}(n) = \psi_{N_j}(n - k N_j),
  \]
  where \( k \) ranges so that the support \( [n - k N_j + 1, n - k N_j + N_j] \) lies within the domain.

This construction is analogous to the Haar wavelet but replaces the constant ±1 with the Möbius function.

---

### 2. Properties of the Mertens Wavelet

#### Orthogonality
- Within the same scale \( j \), wavelets with non-overlapping supports are orthogonal because their supports are disjoint.
- Across scales, wavelets are **not orthogonal** due to overlapping supports and the correlation of \( \mu \). Thus, this is not an orthogonal wavelet basis; biorthogonal or frame representations may be needed.

#### Decay
- \( \psi_N(n) \) has mean 0 and, under heuristic randomness of \( \mu \), variance \( \sim \frac{6}{\pi^2} N \). Hence, amplitude scales as \( O(\sqrt{N}) \). The wavelet does not decay spatially; it is supported on intervals of length \( N \).

#### Fourier Decomposition
The discrete-time Fourier transform of \( \psi_N(n) \) is:
\[
\widehat{\psi}_N(\omega) = \sum_n \psi_N(n) e^{-i\omega n} = \left( \sum_{k=1}^N e^{-i\omega k} \right) \sum_m \mu(m) e^{-i\omega m}.
\]
The sum \( \sum_m \mu(m) e^{-i\omega m} \) is related to \( 1/\zeta(1+i\omega) \) in a distributional sense. Thus, the frequency behavior is tied to the zeros of the zeta function, leading to slow decay and poor frequency localization.

### 3. Compression on Synthetic Signals with Multiplicative Structure

The "Mertens transform" can be defined via **Dirichlet convolution**:
\[
y(n) = (\mu * x)(n) = \sum_{d \mid n} \mu(d) \, x(n/d),
\]
which is invertible by \( x(n) = (1 * y)(n) = \sum_{d \mid n} y(d) \). This is analogous to multiplying the Dirichlet series by \( 1/\zeta(s) \).

#### Synthetic Signal Test
Consider a signal with multiplicative structure: \( x(n) = n^{i\omega} \) for some \( \omega \in \mathbb{R} \). Its Mertens transform is:
\[
y(n) = n^{i\omega} \prod_{p \mid n} (1 - p^{-i\omega}),
\]
which remains dense (not sparse). Thus, the Mertens transform does **not** sparsify such pure multiplicative tones.

However, if \( x \) is already "smooth" in the Dirichlet sense—e.g., \( x = \mu \) itself—then the transform yields a single spike (at \( n=1 \)), achieving perfect sparsity. More generally, signals whose Dirichlet series are rational functions of \( \zeta(s) \) may be sparsified.

**Conclusion:** The Mertens transform compresses signals arising from number-theoretic convolutions (e.g., those with few prime factors) but not generic multiplicative signals. For typical time-series (e.g., stock returns, EEG), it is unlikely to outperform standard wavelets unless the signal has hidden arithmetic structure.

### 4. Mertens Matrix for Compressed Sensing: RIP Analysis

Define an \( m \times n \) matrix \( A \) with entries:
\[
A_{i,j} = \mu(i + j), \quad i=1,\dots,m,\; j=1,\dots,n.
\]
Normalize so that columns have unit norm. For RIP, we need for all \( k \)-sparse \( x \):
\[
(1-\delta) \|x\|_2^2 \leq \|A x\|_2^2 \leq (1+\delta) \|x\|_2^2.
\]

#### Gram Matrix Analysis
The Gram matrix \( G = A^\top A \) has entries:
\[
G_{j,\ell} = \sum_{i=1}^m \mu(i+j) \mu(i+\ell).
\]
- For \( j = \ell \): \( G_{j,j} \approx \frac{6}{\pi^2} m \), since \( \mu(n)^2 \) indicates squarefree numbers with density \( 6/\pi^2 \).
- For \( j \neq \ell \): with lag \( h = |\ell - j| \), the correlation \( \sum_{i=1}^m \mu(i) \mu(i+h) \) is \( O(m / \exp(c\sqrt{\log m})) \) under RH, hence small relative to \( m \) for large \( m \).

Thus, \( G \approx \frac{6}{\pi^2} m \, I \), suggesting \( A \) acts as an approximate isometry.

#### Deterministic RIP?
The above relies on heuristic randomness of \( \mu \). Without unproven assumptions (e.g., Chowla’s conjecture), we cannot guarantee that off-diagonal correlations are uniformly small for all \( k \)-subsets. Known results on sums of \( \mu(n)\mu(n+h) \) are not strong enough to prove RIP with explicit constants. Moreover, for deterministic constructions, RIP typically requires algebraic structure (e.g., chirp matrices), which \( \mu \) lacks.

**Verdict:** The Mertens matrix \( A_{i,j} = \mu(i+j) \) is unlikely to yield deterministic RIP without breakthroughs in analytic number theory. It may satisfy RIP empirically if \( \mu \) behaves randomly, but this is not proven.

### 5. Connection to Reed-Muller Codes and Walsh-Hadamard Transform

Reed-Muller codes and the Walsh-Hadamard transform are based on the **Boolean lattice** \( \{0,1\}^n \), where Möbius inversion corresponds to inclusion-exclusion.

The set of squarefree integers up to \( N \) forms a Boolean lattice (by prime factors), so Reed-Muller codes can be seen as a special case of codes on divisor lattices. For general integers, the divisor lattice is a product of chains (for each prime power), and Möbius inversion still applies. This suggests a generalization: define a "divisor Fourier transform" for functions on \( \{1,\dots,N\} \) by convolution with \( \mu \), analogous to the Walsh-Hadamard transform. Codes based on this transform could be studied, potentially leading to new error-correcting codes with number-theoretic structure.

---

### Summary

1. **Mertens wavelet:** Defined as \( \psi_N(n) = M(n+N) - M(n) \). It is not orthogonal, has \( O(\sqrt{N}) \) amplitude, and slow frequency decay.
2. **Compression:** The Mertens transform (Dirichlet convolution with \( \mu \)) sparsifies signals with arithmetic structure (e.g., sparse in prime factors) but not general multiplicative signals.
3. **RIP for Mertens matrix:** Under heuristic randomness of \( \mu \), the matrix \( A_{i,j} = \mu(i+j) \) may satisfy RIP for large \( m \), but a deterministic proof is out of reach with current number theory. Thus, it does not solve the open problem of deterministic RIP matrices.
4. **Reed-Muller generalization:** The divisor lattice offers a natural generalization of the Boolean lattice, and Möbius inversion provides a Fourier-like transform, suggesting a new class of number-theoretic codes.

These explorations reveal that while the Mertens function has intriguing properties for signal processing, its deterministic and irregular nature limits its utility in traditional frameworks like wavelet bases or compressed sensing. However, it may find niche applications in coding theory and for signals with intrinsic arithmetic structure.
