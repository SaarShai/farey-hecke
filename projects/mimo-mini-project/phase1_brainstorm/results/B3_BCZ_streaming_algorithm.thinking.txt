## Background

### BCZ Cocycle

The Boca-Cobeli-Zaharescu cocycle arises in the study of the geodesic flow on the modular surface $\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})/\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})$. The key objects:

1. The Gauss map $G: x \mapsto \{1/x\}$ on $(0,1)$ — this is the "continued fraction" map.
2. The Farey sequence and its connection to the modular group.
3. The BCZ cocycle gives an explicit cocycle representation where each step corresponds to a Möbius transformation related to consecutive Farey fractions.

The cocycle $A(x) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & \lfloor 1/x \rfloor \end{pmatrix}$ is the standard continued fraction cocycle over the Gauss map. The BCZ contribution is more refined — they study the distribution of Farey fractions via the horocycle flow and obtain precise equidistribution results.

Key properties:
- The Gauss map is exponentially mixing (this is classical, due to Ruelle, Parry, Pollicott).
- The invariant measure is the Gauss measure $\mu(A) = \frac{1}{\log 2} \int_A \frac{dx}{1+x}$.
- The cocycle over the Gauss map has exponential decay of correlations.

### Streaming Algorithms

Streaming algorithms process a sequence $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$ in a single pass, using limited memory. Key primitives:

1. **Hashing**: Map elements to a fixed range uniformly. Needs to be fast and produce "random-looking" outputs.
2. **Count estimation (Misra-Gries)**: Estimate frequencies using $O(1/\varepsilon)$ counters.
3. **Distinct counting (HyperLogLog)**: Estimate $|\{x_1, \ldots, x_n\}|$ using hash-based bit patterns.
4. **Heavy hitters**: Find elements with frequency $> \phi n$.

## Deeper Look at BCZ

The BCZ paper (Boca, Cobeli, Zaharescu, ~2000) studies the distribution of Farey fractions $\{p/q : \gcd(p,q)=1, 0 < p/q < 1\}$ ordered by denominator $q \leq N$. The key result is about the distribution of gaps between consecutive Farey fractions, connected to the horocycle flow on $\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})/\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})$.

The BCZ cocycle is a specific cocycle over the Gauss map that encodes the "jump" from one Farey fraction to the next. In their formulation, if you're at a point $(a, b)$ representing some state on the modular surface, the cocycle gives you the next point explicitly.

The exponential mixing of the horocycle flow (proved by Sarnak, Zagier, and others using spectral theory of automorphic forms) gives quantitative equidistribution.

For streaming purposes, the key property is: if you apply the cocycle repeatedly, the resulting distribution converges exponentially fast to the invariant (Haar) measure. The rate is controlled by the spectral gap of the Laplacian on the modular surface.
