### B. Hecke L-functions over $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-23})$

This is a beautiful problem in algebraic number theory. The class number of $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-23})$ is $h(-23) = 3$. The class group $Cl(K) \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$.

**1. The Three Hecke L-functions**
Let $\psi_0, \psi, \bar{\psi}$ be the characters of $Cl(K)$, where $\psi_0$ is the trivial character.
The three Hecke L-functions are:
*   $L_K(s, \psi_0) = \zeta_K(s)$ (The Dedekind zeta function of $K$).
*   $L_K(s, \psi)$ and $L_K(s, \bar{\psi})$.

By Class Field Theory, these correspond to the 1-dimensional Artin representations of $Gal(H/K)$, where $H$ is the Hilbert Class Field. The field $H$ is generated by the roots of $x^3 - x - 1 = 0$, which has discriminant $-23$. 
Pushed down to $\mathbb{Q}$, the non-trivial Hecke L-functions $L_K(s, \psi)$ and $L_K(s, \bar{\psi})$ are actually conjugate roots of a single **Degree 2 Artin L-function** over $\mathbb{Q}$. This is the L-function of the 2-dimensional irreducible representation of $S_3$ (the Galois group of $x^3-x-1$).

**2. Formula for $a_p$**
For the 2D Artin representation over $\mathbb{Q}$ (which corresponds to tracking the Hecke characters over $K$), the coefficients $a_p$ for $p \neq 23$ depend on how $p$ splits in $K$:
*   **If $\left(\frac{-23}{p}\right) = -1$:** $p$ is inert in $K$. $a_p = 0$.
*   **If $\left(\frac{-23}{p}\right) = 1$:** $p$ splits into $\mathfrak{p} \bar{\mathfrak{p}}$ in $K$. The ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ belongs to one of the 3 ideal classes, say $C^k$ (where $C$ is a generator of $Cl(K)$). 
    *   For the Dedekind zeta function $\zeta_K(s)$, $a_p = 2$.
    *   For the 2D Artin L-function associated with the Hecke characters, the trace of the Frobenius is $a_p = \psi(C) + \bar{\psi}(C) = 2 \cos(2\pi k / 3)$.
    *   Therefore, $a_p \in \{2, -1, -1\}$ depending on which ideal class $p$ splits into.

### C. MUSIC vs Prony vs ESPRIT vs Matrix Pencil

In your prime-bias signal model, you are estimating frequencies $f_j = \gamma_j / 2\pi$ from a finite window $X \in [X_{min}, X_{max}]$. Let $T = \log(X_{max}/X_{min})$ be the time-window, and $N \approx 200$ be the sample count.

**Theoretical Comparison:**
1.  **MUSIC (Schmidt / Stoica & Nehorai 1989):** Exploits the orthogonality of the signal and noise subspaces. Stoica & Nehorai proved that MUSIC is statistically consistent and achieves the Cramer-Rao Bound (CRB) asymptotically as $N \to \infty$. However, MUSIC requires a 1D spectral search (a grid search over $\gamma$), which is computationally heavy but yields high-resolution pseudo-spectra.
2.  **ESPRIT (Roy & Kailath 1989):** Utilizes the shift-invariance of the signal subspace. It directly computes frequencies via eigenvalues of the cross-correlation of array sub-matrices. Asymptotically, ESPRIT achieves the exact same variance as MUSIC (both hit the CRB), but ESPRIT is computationally faster because it avoids the grid search.
3.  **Matrix Pencil / Prony (Hua & Sarkar 1990):** Prony's classical method is highly sensitive to noise. The Matrix Pencil Method (MPM) solves this by utilizing an SVD of a Hankel matrix. Hua & Sarkar showed that for single-snapshot or extremely low-SNR scenarios, MPM can outperform ESPRIT.

**Recommendation for your regime ($T \approx 5, N \approx 200$):**
You have an over-sampled signal ($N=200$ is plenty for the low-lying zeros). 
*   **For pure parameter extraction:** Use **ESPRIT** (or its damped equivalent if you want to model $\beta \neq 1/2$). It achieves the CRB, avoids gridding artifacts, and $N=200$ is large enough that the SVD of the $100 \times 100$ covariance matrix is fast and numerically stable.
*   **For model order selection (How many zeros?):** MUSIC is superior. The gap in the eigenvalue spectrum of the autocorrelation matrix cleanly reveals the number of zeros $M$ present in the window $T$. Use AIC or MDL on the MUSIC eigenvalues to find $M$, then pass $M$ to ESPRIT to extract the $\gamma_j$.

### D. Failure Modes and Boundaries

MUSIC (and all Fourier-based subspace methods) applied to the explicit formula $\psi(X) \approx \sum X^{\rho} / \rho$ will fail under specific, mathematically provable boundaries.

**1. The Rayleigh Resolution Limit (Closely-spaced zeros)**
Because you are sampling a finite window $T = \log(X_{max}/X_{min})$, you cannot resolve zeros closer than the Rayleigh limit. 
$$ \Delta \gamma_{min} \approx \frac{2\pi}{T} $$
For $T = 5$, $\Delta \gamma_{min} \approx 1.256$. 
*   **Boundary:** For the Riemann zeta function, the average spacing between zeros at height $\gamma$ is $\approx 2\pi / \log(\gamma/2\pi)$. When average spacing drops below $1.256$, MUSIC fails completely to separate individual zeros. This happens when $\log(\gamma/2\pi) \approx 5$, meaning **MUSIC will fail for $\zeta$ zeros above $\gamma \approx 950$**. 
*   *Prediction:* For Sym$^4$ $\Delta$ (degree $d=5$), the zeros are 5x denser. MUSIC will fail to resolve Sym$^4$ zeros above $\gamma \approx 50$.

**2. Aliasing / High Zeros (The Nyquist Limit)**
You are sampling the log-domain uniformly with step size $\Delta t = T / N$. The maximum unaliased frequency (the $\gamma$ value) is determined by the Nyquist criterion:
$$ \gamma_{max} < \frac{\pi}{\Delta t} = \frac{\pi N}{T} $$
For $T=5, N=200$, $\gamma_{max} < 125.6$. Any zero with $\gamma > 125$ will alias into lower frequencies and catastrophically corrupt your MUSIC spectrum. 

**3. High Conductor / Truncation Error SNR**
The explicit formula contains terms $\sum_{p, k} \frac{\log p}{p^{k/2}} e^{i k \gamma \log p}$. This acts as "noise" to the $X^{\rho}$ "signal". 
The amplitude of a signal component is roughly $1/\gamma$. The background prime-noise is roughly $O(1)$. 
*   **Boundary:** When $1/\gamma < \sigma_{noise}$, the zero is undetectable. This fundamentally limits low-SNR L-functions (like those with massive conductors $q \gg 1$, where the explicit formula is polluted by $\log q$) from being resolved unless $X_{max}$ is exponentially large.

**4. The Subspace Collapse (High Sym$^k$)**
As $k$ increases, the degree of the L-function increases ($d = k+1$). The autocorrelation matrix must have a dimension $M > d \times (\text{number of zeros in window})$. If $M$ approaches $N/2$, the SVD estimation of the noise subspace fails due to matrix rank deficiency. High symmetric powers will quickly over-saturate the $N=200$ sample capacity.
