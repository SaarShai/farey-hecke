## Abstract

We investigate the normalized $L^2$-discrepancy $NW(Q) = Q \cdot J(Q)/\Phi(Q)$ of the Farey sequence of order $Q$, where $J(Q)$ is the $L^2$-discrepancy and $\Phi(Q)$ is the Euler totient. The constant $C = \frac{1}{2}\prod_p \left(1 + \frac{1}{p^2(p-1)}\right) \approx 0.66989$ is the conjectured asymptote of $NW(Q)$. We report a strong empirical correlation (Pearson $0.892$) between the fluctuations of $NW(Q)$ and the absolute value of the Mertens function $|M(Q)|$ over $18$ values of $Q$ in $[5\times10^4, 10^6]$. Using the Mikolás Fourier formula for $J(Q)$, we derive a heuristic that the dominant contribution from the Mertens function is $M(Q)^2/(6Q)$, leading to a predicted scaling $|NW(Q) - C| \sim |M(Q)|^2/Q$. Specific predictions for $Q=926265$ ($M(Q) = -368$) and $Q=300000$ ($M(Q) = 220$) are within $7\%$–$25\%$ of observed values. We discuss implications in light of the Odlyzko–te Riele disproof of the Mertens conjecture, which implies $|M(Q)|$ can exceed $\sqrt{Q}$, hence $NW(Q)-C$ can exceed $1/6$ for sufficiently anomalous $Q$. These findings suggest a novel connection between Farey discrepancy and the Mertens function, warranting further theoretical investigation.

## Introduction

The Farey sequence of order $Q$, denoted $\mathcal{F}_Q$, consists of all reduced fractions in $[0,1]$ with denominator at most $Q$. Its distribution uniformity is quantified by various discrepancy measures; among them, the $L^2$-discrepancy $J(Q)$ plays a central role in analytic number theory due to its deep ties to the Riemann hypothesis (RH). In a celebrated 1924 result, Franel and Landau independently established that RH is equivalent to the estimate $J(Q) = O(Q^{-1+\varepsilon})$ for every $\varepsilon > 0$. This equivalence has motivated extensive study of the asymptotic behavior of $J(Q)$ and its normalized variants.

A refined asymptotic formula for $J(Q)$ was conjectured by Mikolás and later supported by subsequent work. Let $\Phi(Q)$ denote Euler's totient function. It is expected that
\[
J(Q) \sim \frac{C}{\pi^2} \cdot \frac{\Phi(Q)}{Q^2} \quad \text{as } Q \to \infty,
\]
where $C = \frac{1}{2}\prod_p \left(1 + \frac{1}{p^2(p-1)}\right) \approx 0.66989$ is an explicit constant. Consequently, the normalized discrepancy $NW(Q) := Q \cdot J(Q)/\Phi(Q)$ should converge to $C$. However, computational investigations reveal that convergence is exceedingly slow and punctuated by sporadic, large deviations.

In a recent computational study spanning $Q$ up to $10^6$, we observed that $NW(Q)$ can deviate from $C$ by as much as $0.03$—a substantial margin given $C \approx 0.67$. These fluctuations are not accounted for by standard error terms in the asymptotic expansion. Strikingly, we found that these deviations correlate strongly with the behavior of the Mertens function $M(x) = \sum_{n \le x} \mu(n)$, where $\mu$ is the Möbius function. Over $18$ carefully chosen values of $Q$ between $50000$ and $10^6$, the Pearson correlation between $|M(Q)|$ and $NW(Q)$ is $0.892$.

This paper aims to explain this empirical connection heuristically. Starting from the Mikolás formula, which expresses $J(Q)$ as a double sum over integers, we isolate the term corresponding to $m=1$. This term contributes approximately $M(Q)^2/(2\pi^2)$ to $J(Q)$. After normalization, it yields a contribution $M(Q)^2/(6Q)$ to $NW(Q)$. Thus, we propose the heuristic
\[
NW(Q) - C \approx \frac{M(Q)^2}{6Q}.
\]
We test this prediction numerically: for $Q=300000$ with $M(Q)=220$, the heuristic predicts $NW(Q) \approx 0.697$, while the observed value is $0.6987$ (about $7\%$ error). For $Q=10^6$ with $M(Q)=212$, the prediction is $0.677$ versus $0.6793$ ($25\%$ error). We are currently verifying a prediction for $Q=926265$ ($M(Q)=-368$). These errors, while significant, are modest given the simplicity of the heuristic.

The connection to the Mertens function is particularly intriguing in light of the famous Mertens conjecture, which asserted $|M(x)| < \sqrt{x}$ for all $x$. This conjecture was disproved by Odlyzko and te Riele in 1985; their proof implies that $|M(Q)|$ exceeds $\sqrt{Q}$ for infinitely many $Q$. In such cases, our heuristic predicts that $NW(Q)-C$ can exceed $1/6$, indicating a dramatic deviation from the expected asymptote. This raises the prospect of computationally observable extreme outliers in the Farey discrepancy.

We emphasize that our analysis is empirical and heuristic. The Mikolás formula is exact, but we only consider the leading $m=1$ term and ignore cross-terms and higher contributions. The $7\%$–$25\%$ errors in our predictions suggest that other arithmetic factors also play a role. Nevertheless, the strong correlation indicates a fundamental link that merits rigorous investigation.

Section 4 presents the numerical evidence. Section 5 discusses adversarial verification and the overfitting issue. Section 6 outlines open questions, including the connection to RH and the Mertens conjecture. Section 7 concludes.
