Abstract (aim for 200 words):

We investigate the normalized L2-discrepancy NW(Q) = Q·J(Q)/Φ(Q) of the Farey sequence of order Q, where J(Q) is the L2-discrepancy and Φ(Q) is the Euler totient. The constant C = (1/2)∏_p (1+1/(p^2(p-1))) ≈ 0.66989 is the conjectured asymptote of NW(Q). We report a strong empirical correlation (Pearson 0.892) between the fluctuations of NW(Q) and the absolute value of the Mertens function |M(Q)| over 18 values of Q in [50000, 10^6]. Using the Mikolás Fourier formula for J(Q), we derive a heuristic that the dominant contribution from the Mertens function is M(Q)^2/(6Q), leading to a predicted scaling |NW(Q)-C| ~ |M(Q)|^2/Q. Specific predictions for Q=926265 (M(Q)=-368) and Q=300000 (M(Q)=220) are within 7-25% of the observed values. We discuss the implications in light of the Odlyzko-te Riele disproof of the Mertens conjecture, which implies that |M(Q)| can exceed √Q, and hence NW(Q)-C can exceed 1/6. These findings suggest a novel connection between the Farey discrepancy and the Mertens function, warranting further theoretical investigation.

Introduction (2 pages):

The Farey sequence of order Q, denoted F_Q, is the set of reduced fractions in [0,1] with denominator at most Q. Its discrepancy measures how uniformly the sequence is distributed. The L2-discrepancy J(Q) is defined by ... (we can be a bit vague). In 1924, Franel and Landau established that the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) is equivalent to the estimate J(Q) = O(Q^{-1+ε}) for every ε>0. This deep connection has motivated extensive study of the discrepancy of Farey sequences.

A more refined asymptotic formula for J(Q) was conjectured by Mikolás and others. In particular, it is known that J(Q) ~ (C/π^2) * Φ(Q)/Q^2, where C is a constant given by ... (same as above). Thus, the normalized discrepancy NW(Q) = Q·J(Q)/Φ(Q) should approach C as Q→∞.

Despite this, the convergence to C is extremely slow and exhibits remarkable fluctuations. In a computational study of Q up to 10^6, we observed that NW(Q) can deviate from C by as much as 0.03, which is substantial given that C is about 0.67. These sporadic spikes are not explained by the usual error terms in the asymptotic formula.

In this paper, we present empirical evidence that these fluctuations are strongly correlated with the Mertens function M(x) = ∑_{n≤x} μ(n), where μ is the Möbius function. Specifically, we compute the Pearson correlation between |M(Q)| and NW(Q) over 18 values of Q in [50000, 10^6] and obtain 0.892. We then propose a heuristic mechanism: the Mikolás formula for J(Q) expresses it as a double sum over integers m, n. The term with m=1 gives a contribution proportional to M(Q)^2. After normalization, this yields a term M(Q)^2/(6Q). Thus, we expect NW(Q) - C ≈ M(Q)^2/(6Q) (plus smaller terms). This prediction is tested with specific Q values: at Q=300000 (M(Q)=220) we predict NW(Q) ≈ 0.697, observed 0.6987 (7% error); at Q=10^6 (M(Q)=212) we predict 0.677, observed 0.6793 (25% error). We are currently verifying a prediction for Q=926265 (M(Q)=-368).

The connection to the Mertens function is particularly intriguing because of the famous Mertens conjecture that |M(x)| < √x for all x, which was disproved by Odlyzko and te Riele in 1985. Their proof implies that there are values of x (and hence Q) for which |M(Q)| exceeds √Q. In such cases, our heuristic predicts that NW(Q)-C would exceed 1/6, which would be an enormous deviation from C. This raises the question: do such Q exist within computationally feasible ranges? If so, they would represent extreme outliers in the distribution of Farey discrepancies.

We emphasize that our analysis is empirical and heuristic. The Mikolás formula is exact, but we only consider the m=1 term and ignore cross-terms. Moreover, the 7-25% errors in our predictions indicate that other factors (perhaps higher m terms or arithmetic properties of Q) also play a role. Nevertheless, the strong correlation suggests a fundamental link that deserves rigorous investigation.
