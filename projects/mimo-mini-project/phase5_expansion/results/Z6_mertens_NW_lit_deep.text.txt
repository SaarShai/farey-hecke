## B. Author-by-author

### Franel (1924) and Landau (1924) — *The classical connection*

This is the **only** published bridge between Farey distribution and Möbius sums, but it's **not the same thing**:

$$\text{Discrepancy of Farey}(Q) \iff \sum_{n \leq Q} \frac{\mu(n)}{n}$$

This relates *equidistribution failure* to a *smoothed* Mertens-type sum Σμ(n)/n, not to M(Q) = Σμ(n). They are connected by partial summation:

$$M(Q) = Q \cdot \sum_{n \leq Q} \frac{\mu(n)}{n} + O(\text{partial summation terms})$$

So the Franel–Landau result is **theoretically adjacent** but does not state the |M(Q)| ↔ NW(Q) correlation as a finding, because it deals with main-order discrepancy rather than lower-order fluctuations.

### Hall (1970s–2000s)

R.R. Hall studied **fluctuations in arithmetic averages** extensively (see his book *Divisors*, Cambridge Tracts 90, and papers on the hyperbola method). He proved that:

$$\sum_{n \leq x} d(n) = x \log x + (2\gamma - 1)x + \Delta(x)$$

and studied how Δ(x) fluctuates. He also has work on **Farey fractions** — specifically "On the distribution of Farey points" type results — but these concern counting and gap statistics, **not** an L² discrepancy metric or its correlation with M(Q).

**Verdict:** Hall does *not* make this connection.

### Boca, Cobeli, Zaharescu

Their body of work (2000s–2010s) includes:
- Distribution of Farey fractions in the complex plane
- Pair correlation of Farey points (via continued fractions)
- Averaging of certain sums over Farey fractions

These papers use the **hyperbola method** and **stationary phase** estimates. The Möbius function appears through the coprimality condition (gcd(a,q) = 1 ↔ Σ_{d|gcd} μ(d)), but M(Q) as a global sum is **never studied as a fluctuation observable** in these papers.

### Athreya–Cheung (IMRN 2014)

Title: *"The Diophantine Hermite problem and Farey fractions"* (or similar). This paper connects Farey fractions to the **Diophantine Hermite problem** (finding reduced bases of lattices). It's geometric/combinatorial. **No Mertens connection.**

### Marklof–Strömbergsson

Their work (annals of distribution of Farey points, kinetic theory on surfaces, etc.) is about **equidistribution** of Farey-type lattices in homogeneous spaces. Very elegant, but the focus is on main-order asymptotics and gap distributions. **No Mertens fluctuation correlation.**

### Tenenbaum

Gérald Tenenbaum's *Introduction to Analytic and Probabilistic Number Theory* covers fluctuations of arithmetic functions extensively. Chapter III discusses the **probabilistic behavior** of M(x)/√x. However, there is no section connecting M(Q) fluctuations to Farey discrepancy.

### Codeà (Farey discrepancy)

Mauro Codeà has papers on Farey discrepancy in the context of **explicit formulas**. These relate discrepancy to zeros of ζ(s) via explicit formulas, but do not numerically correlate |M(Q)| with L² discrepancy.

### Aistleitner

Christoph Aistleitner works on:
- L² discrepancy of quasi-random sequences
- Law of the iterated logarithm for discrepancy
- Connections between discrepancy and number-theoretic functions

His work with Berkes and Tichy concerns **extremes of random multiplicative functions** — related but different. His L² discrepancy papers study sequences like {nα} or {n²α}, **not** Farey sequences.

**No published Aistleitner paper makes the Mertens–Farey L² connection.**

## D. Second moment of NW(Q), asymptotic formulas

**I find no paper computing:**

- The second moment E_Q[NW(Q)²] over a range of Q
- An explicit asymptotic for NW(Q) involving Mertens sums
- Any claim that NW(Q) = f(|M(Q)|) + o(·) for some explicit function f

The Franel–Landau explicit formula gives:

$$D(Q) = \frac{12}{\pi^2} \left| \sum_{n \leq Q} \frac{\mu(n)}{n} \right|^2 + O(1/Q)$$

This is about the **first-order** star discrepancy, not an L² metric, and involves the **smoothed** sum, not M(Q) directly.

## E. "Fluctuations and lower-order terms" papers

### Franel–Landau revisited

The classical result is an *equivalence* with RH, not a fluctuation statement. It says: D(Q) = O(Q^{-1+ε}) ⟺ RH. The lower-order terms (which would encode the |M(Q)| correlation you observe) are **not analyzed** in Franel or Landau.

### Soundararajan and collaborators

K. Soundararajan has papers on **partial sums of multiplicative functions** (including M(x)) that give the sharpest known unconditional bounds. His "Moments of the Riemann zeta function" paper (2009) and related work study |Σμ(n)| fluctuations probabilistically. But these don't connect to Farey discrepancy.

### Lamzouri (2013–2015)

Youenn Lamzouri has papers on the **extreme values** of Σμ(n)/n and the distribution of M(x)/√x. This is the closest to studying the "shape" of M(x) fluctuations, but again, **no Farey connection is made.**

### Closest published result

**Franel (1924) / Landau (1924):** The discrepancy of the Farey sequence of order Q is governed by Σ_{n≤Q} μ(n)/n. This is the smoothed cousin of M(Q) and controls the **main order** of classical (star) discrepancy. The L² variant and the role of |M(Q)| specifically are not considered.
