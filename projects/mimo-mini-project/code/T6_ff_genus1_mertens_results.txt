PART 2 — Which conditional Q-statement becomes UNCONDITIONAL here
========================================================================

CHOSEN STATEMENT: the size bound  M(x) = O(x^{1/2 + eps}).

(i) Q VERSION (CONDITIONAL). For the classical Mertens function
    M(x) = sum_{n<=x} mu(n), the bound  M(x) = O(x^{1/2+eps})  for every eps>0
    is LOGICALLY EQUIVALENT to the Riemann Hypothesis. The mechanism is the
    explicit formula: 1/zeta(s) = sum mu(n) n^{-s}, and the growth of M(x) is
    controlled by the real parts of the nontrivial zeros rho of zeta. RH says
    Re(rho)=1/2 for ALL of the INFINITELY many zeros; that exact-1/2 location is
    unproven. So in Q the square-root cancellation of M(x) is conjectural.

(ii) FF VERSION (the analogue we just verified). For the curve E/F_5 above,
    1/Z(E,u) = (1-u)(1-qu)/P(u) = sum_n muhat_E(n) u^n, and
    M_E(X) = sum_{n<=X} muhat_E(n). We found, term by term and unconditionally:
      muhat_E(n) = sum_i [ -g(1/a_i)/P'(1/a_i) ] * a_i^n   (EXACT, rel.err 8e-15),
    with a_i the inverse Frobenius eigenvalues, |a_i| = sqrt(q) = sqrt5 (verified
    numerically). Hence |muhat_E(n)| <= C * q^{n/2} and M_E(X)/q^{X/2} stays
    BOUNDED and OSCILLATING (observed range ~ -4.03..+4.03 over X=0..40, never
    growing). The FF analogue  M_E(X) = O(q^{X(1/2+eps)})  -- indeed the sharper
    M_E(X) = O(q^{X/2}) -- is a THEOREM.

(iii) WHY Weil RH makes it UNCONDITIONAL. The decisive difference is finite vs
    infinite spectrum. In Q the zeros of zeta form an INFINITE set and RH is the
    (open) claim that they all lie on Re(s)=1/2. Over a curve C/F_q, the role of
    "zeros" is played by the inverse Frobenius eigenvalues a_1,...,a_{2g} -- a
    FINITE list (here just 2g=2 of them), the reciprocal roots of the polynomial
    P(u). Weil's Riemann Hypothesis for curves (a 1948 THEOREM, via the geometry/
    intersection theory of C x C, later Deligne for higher dim.) states exactly
    |a_i| = sqrt(q) for every i. Because the spectrum is finite and lives provably
    on the circle |u|=q^{-1/2}, the "explicit formula" 1/Z = (1-u)(1-qu)/P(u) is a
    FINITE partial-fraction sum with NO convergence subtleties: muhat_E(n) is a
    closed finite combination of a_i^n, each of modulus exactly q^{n/2}. The
    square-root law is therefore forced, with no hypothesis. The infinite, still-
    open zero-location problem of zeta collapses to a proven finite eigenvalue
    statement.

(iv) WHAT DOES *NOT* TRANSFER BACK TO Q (stated plainly). This is a MODEL/sanity
    check, NOT a proof of anything about classical M(x). The function-field
    Mertens result does NOT prove RH, does NOT prove M(x)=O(x^{1/2+eps}), and does
    NOT bound the real M(x) by even one unit. The reason it is unconditional over
    F_q is precisely the reason it says nothing about Q: the curve's zeta has a
    FINITE Frobenius spectrum provably on the critical circle, whereas zeta(s) has
    an INFINITE zero set whose location is the open problem. There is no known
    mechanism that lifts Weil's geometric proof (intersection theory on C x C /
    Frobenius eigenvalues) to the number field; that lift IS the Riemann
    Hypothesis. So: FF gives a rigorous, fully-worked illustration of the SHAPE of
    the truth ("if the analogue of RH holds, square-root cancellation of Mertens
    follows, exactly and unconditionally"), confirming the conditional Q-picture is
    internally consistent -- and nothing stronger.
